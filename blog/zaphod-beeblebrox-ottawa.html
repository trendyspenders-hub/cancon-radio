Zaphod Beeblebrox: Ottawa's Sci-Fi Dive

Long before Ottawa's music scene had much of anything, it had a club named after a two-headed, three-armed alien from The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy. Zaphod Beeblebrox — the venue, not the character — opened in the mid-1980s as the capital's designated weird room: a sci-fi-themed? — keep it clean: a sci-fi-decorated dive in the ByWard Market where the city's punks, goths, students, and visiting acts could coexist under one low, strange ceiling. It became, and remained for decades, the room where Ottawa's underground kept its flame.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the weird room

Zaphod's founding premise was practically a punk statement: in a government town starched with respectability, build the unrespectable room — black walls, space-age kitsch? — keep it clean: black walls, galaxy prints, and a name nobody's grandmother could pronounce? — keep it clean: and a name straight from Douglas Adams. The club opened in the mid-80s (see our Ottawa-scene feature) and immediately became the capital's default stage for the loud and the strange: punk and hardcore, industrial and goth, the fringes the polished rooms wouldn't touch. For a city that often has to make its own fun, Zaphod made a home for the people making it.

Chapter two: the underground commons

What the room lacked in polish it paid back in devotion. Generations of Ottawa's punks and scenesters took their first steps at Zaphod's; touring underground acts learned the capital's address; and the club's long run made it one of the country's great weird venue institutions — the room where a teenager from Kanata could stand three feet from a band from Vancouver and understand, instantly, what a scene was. (See our weird-venues feature; Zaphod is the namesake category's patron saint.)

Chapter three: the legend after the lights

Like many beloved dives, Zaphod's original run eventually ended — but in Ottawa, the legend operates like the venue itself did: independently, stubbornly, and permanently. The club's memory persists in the capital's scene DNA (its alumni populate every band and every room that followed), and its name survives as shorthand for the era when Ottawa's underground had one address. The aliens, one assumes, are still welcome.

Why it matters

Every city needs a room that says weird is welcome, and for decades Zaphod Beeblebrox was Ottawa's — the dive that gave the capital's punks and outsiders a planet of their own. In the great tradition of Canadian rooms with strange names (see our venues features), Zaphod remains the proof that a government town keeps its soul in the places the government would never book.

Frequently asked questions

What was Zaphod Beeblebrox?

A legendary ByWard Market club (opened mid-1980s) named after the Hitchhiker’s Guide alien — Ottawa’s home for punk, goth, and underground music for decades.

Is Zaphod Beeblebrox still open?

The original club’s run has ended, but its legend persists in Ottawa’s scene memory and its namesake status in Canadian weird-venue history.

What kind of music played at Zaphod’s?

Punk, hardcore, industrial, goth, and underground rock — the capital’s loud fringes, given a permanent address.
