MuchMusic VJs: Where Are They Now?

They came through our living rooms every afternoon for three decades — cool, funny, music-obsessed, and impossibly approachable. The MuchMusic VJs were more than hosts: they were the country's taste-making roommates, and their post-channel careers have been almost as varied as their playlists. Here's where the era's most beloved VJs landed.

BY CANCON RADIO3 MIN READA STORY

The roster, then and now

  1. Christopher Ward — MuchMusic's first breakout VJ and a genuine songwriter (co-writer of "Black Velvet" — yes, really); went on to a long behind-the-scenes career in music and television writing.
  2. Steve Anthony — the funny one, then and now; a decades-long broadcasting career in Toronto television kept him a household presence long after the channel's peak.
  3. Erica Ehm — the poet-VJ who turned her on-air persona into an entrepreneurial empire — writing, publishing (the Yummy Mummy era), and speaking; the blueprint for VJ-to-brand.
  4. Jeanne Beker — fashion's broadcasting institution: from the MuchMusic trenches to Fashion Television, where she became Canada's most famous fashion journalist.
  5. Rick Campanelli ("Rick the Temp") — won a national contest to become a VJ and stayed for an era; a beloved broadcasting figure whose post-Much career kept him on national screens and airwaves.
  6. Jennifer Hollett — transitioned from VJ to a career in journalism and public life, carrying the music-channel credibility into serious media and civic engagement.
  7. Amanda Walsh — from the MuchMusic desk to a respected acting career (film and television), one of the roster's most successful screen pivots.
  8. George Stroumboulopoulos — MuchMusic's The NewMusic host became a national broadcaster: the CBC flagship eponymous? — keep it clean: the CBC's flagship talk show, hockey broadcasting, and one of the most recognizable voices in Canadian media — a career that began with music journalism on the channel.
  9. Sarain Fox — among the later-generation VJs who carried the channel into its final era; continued as a broadcaster, dancer, and Indigenous arts advocate.
  10. The ensemble — dozens more hosts, reporters, and specialty-show personalities flowed through 299 Queen West, and a remarkable share of them remain in Canadian media, podcasting, and the music business: the channel functioned, among other things, as a finishing school for the country's broadcasters.

Why the VJs mattered

The VJ era was built on a single design decision — hosts as music obsessives, not just announcers — and it created an intimacy television rarely achieves. A VJ's endorsement carried the weight of a friend's recommendation, and their interviews (on the street, in the studio, occasionally chaotic) gave Canadian artists a national platform with personality attached. When the channel faded, the talent didn't vanish; it dispersed into the country's entire media ecosystem, which is why so MuchMusic DNA survives in Canadian broadcasting today.

The lesson

MuchMusic's greatest export wasn't videos — it was people: a generation of broadcasters trained in the channel's street-facing, artist-first ethos. The building's speakers went silent; its alumni are everywhere.

Frequently asked questions

Who was the most famous MuchMusic VJ?

Depends on the era — Christopher Ward and Steve Anthony for the 80s, Erica Ehm and Jeanne Beker as icons, Rick Campanelli for the 90s, and George Stroumboulopoulos for the NewMusic years; each became a household name.

What did MuchMusic VJs do after the channel?

Broadcasting (radio and TV), acting, writing, publishing, entrepreneurship, and public life — the roster dispersed across Canadian media with remarkable success.

Who wrote “Black Velvet,” the Alannah Myles hit?

Christopher Ward — MuchMusic’s first famous VJ — co-wrote the song, one of the great hidden credits in Canadian music history.
