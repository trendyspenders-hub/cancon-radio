CKUA: Alberta's Century-Old Radio Lifeline

Some institutions are old; CKUA (the Alberta radio station on air since 1927 — one of Canada's oldest radio stations) is vital: the community broadcaster that taught the province its music (the folk, the roots, the world — the curriculum of a whole province's musical education), survived a near-death that should have ended it (the 1997 shutdown crisis), and kept broadcasting into its second century. The lifeline, on the timeline:

The timeline

1927the founding

CKUA signs on (the University of Alberta's station — the educational mandate — the extension broadcasting of a young province) with a mission that persists: a station for the province's culture, before Alberta had much of an infrastructure to call its own. The call letters (the "UA" — the University of Alberta's stamp) anchored it through the campus years until the 1970s transfer to provincial access broadcasting.

The Access years (1970–1996)

The station's golden age: the folk boom (see our features) — the folk and roots programming that trained the province (see our Alberta-country feature), CKUA becoming the de facto national broadcaster of Alberta's musical culture (the regional artists' first airplay), the world music curriculum (the global programming before the term was common), and the public-affairs town hall (the province's conscience on the air). The announcers (the long-tenured hosts — the personalities as institutions) became the province's companions — the radio as presence.

1997the shutdown and the resurrection

The crisis (the Klein government's budget cuts — the station's sudden shutdown — the province's fury) nearly ended CKUA — and instead remade it: the listeners (the donors — the thousands — the rescue campaign) bought their station back (the community-ownership model — the nonprofit CKUA Radio Foundation), proving the lesson of the station's irreplaceability. The resurrection shaped everything since: the donor-funded model — the independence as structure.

2000–presentthe second century

The modern CKUA (the continued programming — the roots, folk, and world curriculum intact) has navigated the industry's storms (the digital transition) while keeping the presence (the announcers — the companionship — the Alberta room on the air). The approaching 2027 centennial arrives with the model proven: ninety-seven years of Alberta's room. The lifeline holds. The donors keep it. The province listens.

Why it matters

CKUA matters as the proof that community broadcasting (the listener-owned model) can outlast governments, formats, and every industry convulsion — and because its century of programming trained the province that gave Canadian music some of its finest. The lifeline holds. Alberta's century-old heart, still on the air.

Frequently asked questions

What is CKUA?

Alberta's community radio station (on air since 1927) — folk, roots, world, and public-affairs programming, listener-owned since the 1997 rescue.

What happened to CKUA in 1997?

The provincial government shut it down in a budget cut; a massive donor campaign resurrected it as a nonprofit foundation — the community-ownership model that persists.

Is CKUA still broadcasting?

Yes — from Edmonton and across Alberta (and streaming globally), approaching its 2027 centennial.
