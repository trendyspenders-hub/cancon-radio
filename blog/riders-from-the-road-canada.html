Riders from the Road: The Strangest Green Room Requests

Tucked inside every touring contract is the rider — the band's list of backstage demands: the food, the drinks, the towels, the specific brand of throat lozenge. Canada's rider lore ranges from the practical to the paranoid to the purely petty. The strangest requests from the Canadian circuit:

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READA STORY

The rider lore

  1. The brown M&M principle — the famous tale of the rock colossus whose rider demanded M&Ms with the brown ones removed: the clause functioned not as diva behavior but as a safety check (the band's explanation, long verified: if the venue couldn't read the rider carefully enough to remove brown M&Ms, they couldn't be trusted with the pyrotechnics). Every rider since lives in its shadow.
  2. The Canadian-content rider — the CanCon twist: riders that demand Canadian products — the local craft beer, the "feed us from the city's best restaurant" clause, the poutine requirement for the Montreal date — the demand as regional tribute, the band proving they'd read the map.
  3. The comfort-curve demands — the requirements that scale with success: the van-tour rider (water, sandwiches, a place to sit) escalating to the bus-tour rider (the full spread) and finally the arena rider (the rooms of demands). The rider is the career's progress report, legible to anyone who reads it.
  4. The paranoid provisions — the clauses that seem strange until explained: the no-photo rules, the confidentiality clauses, the specific dressing-room temperature — all hiding a reason (the artist's privacy, the vocalist's health, the lessons of past disasters). The rider's absurdity is usually someone's scar tissue.
  5. The local-hero requests — the Canadian-specific charm: bands that ask for local recommendations in the rider (the rider that requests "the city's best sandwich, chosen by the venue's staff") — the demand as conversation starter, the rider written by people who still like the road.
  6. The practical-to-the-point-of-poetry — the working-band riders: the requests that aren't demands but defenses (the food-allergy clauses, the sobriety-friendly provisions) — the riders as self-care documents, the strange requests that are simply health, on paper.

What riders teach

The rider is the industry's most honest document: the real contract behind the contract — the band's actual needs, fears, and habits, itemized. The strangest entries are never really strange: they're inspection, health, and homesickness, itemized. Read the rider and you can read the career, the health, and the homeward ache of everyone on the stage. The green room is where the mythologies undress. The rider is the wardrobe list.

Frequently asked questions

What is a concert rider?

The band's backstage requirements attached to the performance contract — food, drink, technical needs, and comforts, scaling with the career.

What was the brown M&M clause?

A famous rider demand (a major rock act) requiring M&Ms with the browns removed — functioning as a venue-competence test, not petulance.

Do Canadian bands have strange riders?

Yes — with the national flavor of local-product requests alongside the universal comfort and paranoia provisions.
