Schitt’s Creek's Musical Moments: A Licensing Masterclass

Halfway through Schitt's Creek's run, critics started noticing something odd: a low-budget CBC comedy was spending its music budget like a prestige drama — and every needle-drop worked. By the finale, the show's music had become a subject of study in itself: how Dan and Eugene Levy's little show about a rich family in a motel used songs — original, licensed, and earned — to build the warmest world on television. The masterclass:

BY CANCON RADIO3 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the original-music weapon

The show's secret weapon was its original music: composer Maribeth Solomon's score (with the show's original songs and arrangements) gave Schitt's Creek its sound — jaunty, warm, just-left-of-corny — and the in-universe performances turned music into character. The landmark: "A Little Bit Alexis" (2019) — Alexis Rose's self-produced single, performed by Annie Murphy as a chaotic, confident, oddly endearing pop pastiche that became a genuine cultural phenomenon (streaming, covers, drag performances — the song escaped the show entirely and became a hit on its own terms). The joke was perfect because it was real: a fully produced, plausible-2010s pop single, performed in-character, that worked unironically. That's the show's whole music philosophy in three minutes.

Chapter two: the licensed moments that broke the internet

The licensed needle-drops were chosen with the same care. The series' most famous: Tina Turner's "The Best" — deployed across the series as David and Patrick's song, and detonated in the finale's wedding/flash-mob sequence into one of the most-discussed TV music moments of the decade (the scene's choreography, the crowd's joy, the song's earned recontextualization — from ironic karaoke to genuine anthem — is a case study in how licensing serves story). The show's other uses — the cabaret performances (Moira's theatrical musicality), the Christmas special's tunes, the recurring uses of specific songs as character signatures — followed the same rule: no song appears for a wink; every song means something to somebody in the frame. The budget (modest, by TV standards — keep it light: modest, by TV standards) was spent on fewer, better moments — the discipline that made each one land.

Chapter three: the lesson

Schitt's Creek's music strategy is now the industry standard for how comedy uses songs: originals as character work (the "Alexis" model), licensed tracks as emotional architecture (the "Simply the Best" model), and the score as the show's warm heartbeat underneath. The show's audience — huge, devoted, and listening — proved that audiences notice music when it's treated as story rather than wallpaper (see our sync-licensing feature). And the show's Canadian-ness extended to its music choices — the production's sensibility (see our sync features) kept the selections feeling like the world it depicted: unshowy, warm, and secretly perfect. The little sitcom from CBC built a jukebox that outlasted the show. "A little bit Alexis" indeed.

Why it matters

It matters because Schitt's Creek proved the oldest sync-licensing truth at the highest modern visibility: songs make stories land when they're chosen, not placed. The show's music moments are taught (literally — in film and TV programs) as the model. And the Tina Turner scene remains the decade's definitive argument that a well-earned needle-drop can do what pages of dialogue can't. Simply the best, indeed.

Frequently asked questions

What is “A Little Bit Alexis”?

Alexis Rose’s in-universe pop single (performed by Annie Murphy) from Schitt’s Creek — a genuine streaming hit and cultural phenomenon beyond the show.

What song plays at David and Patrick’s wedding?

Tina Turner’s “The Best” (“Simply the Best”) — deployed across the series as their song and featured in the finale’s celebrated sequence.

Who did Schitt’s Creek’s music?

Composer Maribeth Solomon (score and original music), with the Levys’ curation of licensed tracks — a model of music-as-story now taught in film and TV programs.
