Helix: The Hardest-Working Hair Band in the Country

The 80s hair-metal boom minted dozens of Canadian bands; almost all of them are gone. Helix — the Kitchener, Ontario quintet fronted forever by Brian Vollmer — is not gone. Forty-plus years, thousands of shows, one unbroken work ethic: Helix is the hardest-working hair band in the country, and the story of how they outlasted the era that made them is better than the era itself.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the long apprenticeship (1974–1983)

Helix formed in Kitchener in 1974 — a decade before the hair-metal gold rush, which means they spent the 70s paying the true dues: Ontario's bar circuit, the legion halls and high-school gyms? — keep it clean: the bars, the fairs, the endless southern Ontario circuit, refining the twin-guitar attack and Vollmer's arena-ready howl. By the time the mid-80s wave arrived, Helix wasn't a new band riding a trend — they were veterans with a machine already running, perfectly positioned for the moment.

Chapter two: the platinum moment (1984–1985)

Walkin' the Razor's Edge (1984) detonated: "Rock You" — the anthem of the era's Canadian summer? — keep it clean: the era's Canadian arena anthem — and a string of radio hits ("Gimme Gimme Good Lovin'," "Heavy Metal Love") carried the album to platinum and the band to the majors' full machinery: videos, tours, the whole 80s apparatus at maximum volume. Helix did everything the era asked — bigger hair, bigger stages, bigger everything — while remaining, underneath, the same working band from Kitchener: Vollmer's blue-collar authenticity was the thing the gloss couldn't manufacture, and audiences clocked it.

Chapter three: the long road out

The hair-metal correction arrived on schedule, and Helix — unlike most of the era's acts — simply kept working. The 90s and 2000s became the road-warrior era: festival slots, club gigs, the classic-rock circuit, anniversary tours, and a catalog that kept earning (see our hardest-working-bands feature). The lineup changed around Vollmer's constant center; the work ethic never did. Today Helix is the era's great survivor story — still touring, still loud, still proof that the bands who treat it as a job outlive the bands who treat it as a moment.

Why they matter

Helix matters as the counter-narrative to the hair-metal story: not the cautionary tale of flash-in-the-pan excess but the proof that the era's workhorses became its institutions. "Rock You" still detonates any room it's played in. And somewhere in Ontario, on any given weekend, Helix is loading in — because that's what they do. It's what they've always done.

Frequently asked questions

What was Helix’s biggest hit?

“Rock You” (1984) — the signature anthem from the platinum album Walkin’ the Razor’s Edge.

Where is Helix from?

Kitchener, Ontario — formed in 1974 and fronted ever since by Brian Vollmer.

Is Helix still together?

Yes — Helix continues to tour and record, one of Canada’s hardest-working and longest-running rock bands.
