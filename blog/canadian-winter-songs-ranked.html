Canadian Songs About Winter, Ranked by Frostbite Factor

No country on earth has a deeper winter songbook — and no country needs one more. But not all winter songs are equally cold. Herewith the canon, ranked by frostbite factor — from the gentle snow to the polar apocalypse:

The winter as aspiration — the snowbird escaping south, warmth as the dream (see our Snowbird feature). The gentlest entry in the canon.
  2. "Sundown"? no. #2: "Helpless" — Neil Young (1970). Frostbite factor: deep freeze. "Blue, blue windows behind the stars... this town in north Ontario" — the cold as memory and ache, Young's most Saskatchewan song despite the Ontario reference (see our Young features).

The frostbite verdict

The winter songbook (see our features) covers every register (see our features): the escape (Snowbird, River), the history (Northwest Passage, Edmund Fitzgerald), the cozy (Song for a Winter's Night), the nation (Mon pays), and the wind (Four Strong Winds). No country sings its weather more honestly (see our craft feature). Bundle up. Press play. The cold, at least, is shared.

Frequently asked questions

What is the greatest Canadian winter song?

“River” (Mitchell) and “Northwest Passage” (Rogers) top the critical lists — the heartbreak song and the Arctic epic.

What’s the oldest Canadian winter song?

“The Huron Carol” (1642) — the Jesuit-Huron carol, the canon’s foundation stone.

Is there a Canadian winter playlist?

You’re reading it. Add “Wheat Kings,” “Helpless,” and “Mon pays,” and you have the full frostbite spectrum.
