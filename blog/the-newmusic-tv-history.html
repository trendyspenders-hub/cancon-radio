The NewMusic: Canada's Answer to 60 Minutes for Bands

Before MuchMusic put VJs in the windows, Toronto's Citytv was already treating music as journalism: The NewMusic (1979–2008, with roots earlier), the weekly program that profiled artists, covered the industry, and interviewed musicians with a rigor Canadian television had never bothered to attempt. Its alumni became the country's media establishment; its standards became the industry's reference point. The story of the show that took music seriously:

Chapter one: the Citytv ethos

The NewMusic emerged from Moses Znaimer's Citytv (see our MuchMusic feature for the parent channel's story) with a radical-for-TV premise: music deserved reporting, not just promotion — long-form profiles, industry coverage, and interviews conducted by journalists who'd done their homework. The show's aesthetic matched the channel's street-facing, no-teleprompter? — keep it light: street-facing house style — direct, curious, allergic to fluff — and its timing was perfect: the show grew up alongside the 80s CanCon wave (see our features), giving the era's artists a serious national platform just as the regulations were forcing radio to notice them. The NewMusic didn't just cover the scene; it helped constitute it.

Chapter two: the alumni factory

The show's deepest legacy is its people: generations of Canadian music journalists passed through The NewMusic's doors — most famously J.D. Roberts (the future John Roberts of American network news — the kid who interviewed the era's biggest acts in the 80s? — keep it light: the future American network anchor, who cut his teeth interviewing the era's biggest acts) and George Stroumboulopoulos (the future CBC star — see our VJs feature), among many others who became the country's critics, hosts, and industry voices. The show functioned as Canadian music journalism's finishing school, and its standards — research, access, the long-form profile — set the bar the rest of the field measured itself against for three decades.

Chapter three: the quiet end and the durable standard

The NewMusic's long run wound down in the 2000s, as music television (see our MuchMusic feature) drifted from journalism toward entertainment — the industry's tragic arc in miniature. But the standard it set proved durable: the alumni populate the country's media to this day (see our critics feature), the long-form music profile it pioneered remains the form's gold standard, and its premise — that music is worth serious attention — is, functionally, this website's founding assumption. The show's gone. The homework is still done.

Why it matters

It matters because a culture's music journalism is as load-bearing as its music: The NewMusic built the critical infrastructure that let Canada talk about its own music with rigor, trained the people who'd do the talking for decades, and modeled the premise that a nation's music deserves national attention. The VJs got the fame. The NewMusic did the work.

Frequently asked questions

What was The NewMusic?

A Citytv music-journalism program (1979–2008) — long-form profiles, industry coverage, and serious artist interviews, a national first.

Who hosted The NewMusic?

Its roster of journalists included J.D. Roberts (the future John Roberts of U.S. network news) and George Stroumboulopoulos, among many who shaped Canadian music media.

What happened to The NewMusic?

The long run ended in the 2000s as music TV drifted from journalism toward entertainment — but its alumni and standards remain the field's backbone.
