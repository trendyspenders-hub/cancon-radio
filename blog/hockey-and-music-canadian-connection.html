Hockey and Music: The Surprising Connection

No other country's national sport has a soundtrack the way Canada's does. Hockey doesn't just appear in Canadian music — it structures it: anthems played before every game, goal songs roared by 18,000 voices, and an entire songwriting tradition that uses the rink as a stage for the country's biggest themes (community, work, loss, winter). The game's musical history is deeper than one Stompin' Tom song. Here's the scouting report.

BY CANCON RADIO3 MIN READA STORY

The hockey music canon

  1. "The Hockey Theme" (Dolores Claman, 1968) — The most recognizable melody in the country, full stop. Composed for CBC's Hockey Night in Canada, Claman's instrumental became the sound of Saturday night itself for forty years — and then made headlines in 2008, when a rival broadcaster acquired the rights and the iconic theme changed networks, a genuine national-story news event. A theme song Canadians argued about on the front page: hockey music at its purest.
  2. "The Hockey Song" (Stompin' Tom Connors, 1973) — The third national anthem, arena-tested for five decades (see our Stompin' Tom feature): three verses, one Zamboni, zero wasted words.
  3. "Fifty-Mission Cap" (The Tragically Hip, 1992) — The game's great mystery mythologized: Bill Barilko, the Leafs defenseman who scored the 1951 Cup winner and vanished that summer, plane never found — "the last goal he ever scored won the Leafs the Cup." Written, astonishingly, to make a 1990s kid look up a 1950s tragedy. It worked; it always works.
  4. "Big League" (Tom Cochrane, 1988) — The other side of the dream: a small-town kid with NHL talent and a highway ending. The saddest hockey song ever written, and the truest — which is why minor-arena playlists still can't quit it.
  5. "Wheat Kings" (The Tragically Hip, 1992) — Not technically about hockey — but try telling that to every arena that has ever played it, or to the culture that hears its prairie nightscape as rink-adjacent by default.
  6. Goal songs as folk music — Every arena's goal horn plus chosen anthem (from "Chelsea Dagger" to the retro Canadian picks) is a locally-evolved musical tradition: part chant, part ritual, entirely communal.
  7. The dressing-room pipeline — Where do Canadian musicians and hockey players come from? The same small towns, mostly. The junior-hockey dressing room and the bar-stage circuit are the country's twin finishing schools for performers, and the crossover (players who sing, musicians who skate, everyone who knows someone) is total.
  8. The barn as concert hall — From Stompin' Tom's taverns to charity games with rock-band lineups, the arena has always doubled as Canada's most reliable mid-size venue: sheet in, stage in, same building.

Why hockey and music keep crossing

Both are Canada's great communal performance traditions: learned young, judged publicly, and at their best when the whole room is doing it together. The anthem before the game and the chorus during it are the same act — a crowd proving it knows the words. That's why the connection runs so deep it's nearly invisible: hockey music isn't a genre. It's the sound of the country agreeing on something for three minutes at a time.

Frequently asked questions

Who wrote the Hockey Night in Canada theme?

Dolores Claman, in 1968 — it became the country’s most recognizable instrumental before famously changing networks in 2008.

What are the best Canadian hockey songs?

“The Hockey Song” (Stompin’ Tom), “Fifty-Mission Cap” (the Hip), and “Big League” (Tom Cochrane) form the canonical top three.

Is “Fifty-Mission Cap” a true story?

Yes — Bill Barilko’s 1951 Stanley Cup-winning goal and his disappearance that summer are real, and the Hip’s song remains the reason many Canadians know the story.
