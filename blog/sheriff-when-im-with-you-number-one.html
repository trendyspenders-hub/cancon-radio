Sheriff: "When I'm with You" and the Strangest #1 Ever

The history of Canadian one-hit wonders has one entry stranger than all the rest — a record that hit #1 in America three years after the band that made it had broken up. That band was Sheriff, that record was "When I'm with You," and its second life remains one of the weirdest chart stories of the 1980s: the power ballad that refused to die.

Chapter one: the Toronto band (1979–1985)

Sheriff formed in Toronto in 1979 — a five-piece fronted by the stratospheric? — keep it clean: fronted by singer Freddy Curci, whose glass-shattering range became the band's signature. Their self-titled 1982 debut produced "When I'm with You," a yearning power ballad that Curci's vocal carried into the heavens? — keep it clean: that Curci's vocal carried to operatic heights. In Canada, it was a hit; in the US, it stalled outside the top 50. The band, despite the promise, split in 1985 — the usual collisions of ambition and chemistry — and that, by every law of the industry, should have been that.

Chapter two: the resurrection (1988–1989)

Except: radio wouldn't let it go. American stations kept playing "When I'm with You" — soft-rock and AOR programmers kept the flame — and in 1988, the label, reading the persistence, re-released the single. In February 1989, three years after Sheriff had ceased to exist, "When I'm with You" hit #1 on the Billboard Hot 100. Think about what that means: America's biggest song, performed by a band that no longer existed, from an album six years old, fronted by a singer with no band to tour it. The industry had never quite seen it, and hasn't often seen it since.

Chapter three: the afterlives

The members, inevitably, rode the aftershock in different directions: Curci and keyboardist Steve DeMarchi formed Alias, whose own power ballad ("More Than Words Can Say") kept the late-Curci? — keep it clean: which gave Curci a second chart life — a genuine one-two punch of ballad supremacy from one Toronto band's ashes. Sheriff itself eventually reunited for the classic-rock circuit, where "When I'm with You" functions as the set-closing monument to the strangest #1 of the decade. The song, meanwhile, became eternal: wedding staple, karaoke Everest? — keep it clean: wedding staple and vocal showcase, one of the great Canadian power ballads by accident, persistence, and a voice that could not be argued with.

Why it matters

Sheriff matters because their story is the industry's strangest proof of a simple truth: the audience decides. Labels shelve, bands break up, campaigns end — but if a song keeps getting played, it keeps being alive. "When I'm with You" waited three years for its #1, and when it arrived, it arrived for a band that couldn't even play the gig. The strangest #1 in Canadian history. And one of the best stories we've got.

Frequently asked questions

Did Sheriff’s “When I’m with You” really hit #1 after they broke up?

Yes — the single reached #1 on the Billboard Hot 100 in 1989, roughly three years after the band had dissolved; its album was six years old at the time.

Who sang “When I’m with You”?

Freddy Curci, Sheriff’s Toronto-born frontman — later of Alias (“More Than Words Can Say”).

What happened to Sheriff after the #1?

The song’s success powered afterlife projects (Alias among them), and the band eventually reunited for the classic-rock circuit; the record itself became a permanent power-ballad standard.
