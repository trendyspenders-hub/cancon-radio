Drake's Toronto: A Neighborhood-by-Neighborhood Guide to the 6

No artist in history has name-checked a city as thoroughly as Drake has name-checked Toronto. Streets, intersections, housing projects, high schools, plazas, freeway exits — his catalog functions as a GPS of the city, and understanding the references unlocks the persona. Here's the tour, neighborhood by neighborhood.

BY CANCON RADIO4 MIN READA CITY MAP

Stop one: Forest Hill — "started from the bottom"

Begin where Aubrey Graham grew up: Forest Hill, one of Toronto's wealthiest neighborhoods — which makes "started from the bottom" one of the most argued-over claims in rap. The truth is more interesting than the meme: Drake was raised in a rented half of a Forest Hill mansion, his father a Memphis drummer who left, his mother a teacher. He attended Forest Hill Collegiate, then Vaughan Road Academy. The "bottom" in Drake's telling is less about poverty than about absence — a father gone, a city that had no template for a half-Jewish Canadian kid rapping. The neighborhood matters because his entire mythology is built on the tension between where he lived and what he claims.

Stop two: Weston Road — the spine of the autobiography

If Forest Hill is the wound, Weston Road is the spine. Drake references the northwest-end artery constantly, and it functions as shorthand for the working-class Toronto that raised his sensibility: strip malls, West Indian bakeries, the street's unglamorous realness versus Forest Hill's polish. When he raps about Weston Road, he's rapping about the city's actual texture — the part of Toronto that existed long before the condos, and that he insists the world remember. "Weston Road Flows" is the thesis track: nostalgia as real estate.

Stop three: the 6 — how a nickname conquered the world

The city's adopted global brand — "the 6," the "6ix" — was popularized by Drake, via his OVO label and 2015's If You're Reading This It's Too Late, though the term predates him (Toronto rappers and the 416/647 area codes share credit). The genius was the container: a city's entire identity compressed into one syllable, exportable to Super Bowl introductions and basketball jerseys. Purists grumbled about a private-jet millionaire branding the city; everyone else adopted it within eighteen months. Toronto had never had a shorthand before — not "T-dot," not "Hogtown." Drake gave the city its first globally recognized nickname, and he did it with a digit.

Stop four: OVO Fest — the city as festival

Since 2010, OVO Fest has turned the lakeshore amphitheatre into the year's most star-dense concert weekend — surprise guests routinely including the biggest names in music, because Drake made Toronto a mandatory stop. The festival's deeper function: it inverted the old Toronto complex. For decades, Canadian artists believed you had to leave to matter; OVO Fest proved the biggest artists on earth would come to Toronto to matter. The Raptors championship parade in 2019 — two million people, Drake as global ambassador — was the same argument made in confetti.

Stop five: the Views cover — the CN Tower, finally

The 2016 cover of Views is Drake standing, small, on the CN Tower's maintenance arm — Canada's most famous landmark, usually treated with postcard reverence, here rendered as a throne. It was the visual completion of the project: the city not as backdrop but as body. Every subsequent Toronto rapper, singer, and pop act inherits that framing — the hometown not as something to escape but as the center of the map.

The references, decoded: a pocket glossary

  • "The 6 / 6ix" — Toronto itself; from the 416/647 area codes and the city's boroughs, popularized globally by Drake.
  • "Weston Road" — the northwest artery standing for unglamorous, working-class Toronto authenticity.
  • "Woes" — "running through the 6 with my woes" — working on excellence; his circle.
  • "OVO" — October's Very Own; the label, the brand, the festival, the owl.
  • "Marvins Room" — the studio-late-night mood; a place, a timestamp, a genre.
  • "Degrassi" — where the world first met Aubrey Graham; Drake has never disowned a frame of it.

Frequently asked questions

Why does Drake call Toronto "the 6"?

The term derives from Toronto's 416 area code and its boroughs; Drake popularized it globally around 2015, though Toronto rappers used it before him.

Where did Drake grow up?

Forest Hill, an affluent Toronto neighborhood — a fact that feeds the long-running debate over "started from the bottom," which Drake frames as personal rather than financial.

What is OVO Fest?

Drake's annual Toronto concert festival, running since 2010, famous for secret superstar guests and for making Toronto a destination rather than a tour stop.
