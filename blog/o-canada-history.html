O Canada: A History of the Anthem's Endless Reinventions

Calixa Lavallée wrote "O Canada" in 1880; the country has been reinventing it ever since. The anthem's long afterlife, on the timeline:

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READA TIMELINE

The timeline

1880the birth

Lavallée composes the music for Saint-Jean-Baptiste Day; Adolphe-Basile Routhier writes the French lyrics. It's a Quebec patriotic song, not yet a national anything.

1908–1980the adoption

English lyrics appear (the Robert Stanley Weir version sticks); the song gradually becomes the de facto English-Canadian anthem — sung at hockey games, schools, and graduations for decades before official status. 1980: parliament makes it official.

The reinventions — the arrangement era

The anthem gets swung (jazz versions), rocked (the Guess Who-era garage takes), translated (the French original reasserts itself), and reclaimed (performances in Indigenous languages — see our anthem feature). Every generation re-sings it to prove the song can hold the country.

The 2018 lyric change

"True patriot love in all thy sons command" becomes "in all of us command" — the anthem's first official edit in a century, decades of advocacy compressed into one word.

Why it endures

"O Canada" endures because it accepts reinvention: the melody is sturdy enough for any arrangement, the lyrics elastic enough for any era's politics. The anthem is less a fixed text than a vessel — and the country keeps proving the vessel holds.

Frequently asked questions

Who wrote O Canada?

Calixa Lavallée (music, 1880) and Adolphe-Basile Routhier (French lyrics); Robert Stanley Weir's English lyrics date from 1908.

When did O Canada become the official anthem?

1980 — after decades as the de facto anthem.

Has O Canada been changed?

Yes — the 2018 lyric change to "in all of us command," plus endless unofficial arrangements across genres and languages.
