A Definitive Guide to Canadian Music Slang

Every industry has its shorthand; Canada's music business has a lot of it, much of it inherited from the CanCon era. The definitive guide to the words you hear in Canadian green rooms and label meetings:

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READA Q&A

The terms

CanCon — the regulations (see our features) and, informally, the whole domestic ecosystem: "they're a CanCon act" means Canadian-content-compliant, not corny (though the debate persists).

MAPL — the Music/Artist/Performance/Lyrics test (see our features): "does it MAPL?" means does it qualify for the quota — the industry's favorite pub trivia.

The 6 / the 6ix — Toronto (see our Drake feature): from the 416/647 area codes. Usage note: Drake popularized it; Torontonians remain divided.

The ROC — Rest of Canada: Quebec industry shorthand for everywhere outside the province (see our parallel-universe feature). Slightly pointed.

Factor / FACTOR — the funding body (see our features): "we got FACTORed" means the grant came through — and half the indie records you love exist because of it.

The Junos / the Polaris — the two prizes (see our feature): "Juno-nominated" = commercially validated; "Polaris-shortlisted" = critically canonized.

Support slot — the opening set (see our ladder feature): "we're supporting [headliner]" — the industry's graduation ceremony.

The rider — the backstage demands list (see our feature): "no brown M&Ms" as shorthand for read the contract carefully.

Guarantee vs. door — the two payment structures (see our booking feature): the guarantee is the flat fee; the door is the risk-sharing split. The first question at settlement.

Sync — the screen placement (see our sync feature): "it synced" = the song got placed in a show/ad/film — the industry's most coveted three-letter word after Juno.

Off the floor — recorded live, together (see our feature): the badge of honor for bands who can play.

Catalog — the back of the career (see our streaming feature): "they're a catalog act" = the streaming numbers sustain the tour.

Frequently asked questions

What does MAPL stand for?

Music, Artist, Performance, Lyrics — the two-of-four test for Canadian-content qualification (see our features).

What is “the ROC”?

Rest of Canada — Quebec's shorthand for everywhere outside Quebec (see our features).

What does “synced” mean?

A song has been placed in a film, TV show, commercial, or game — often the most lucrative placement a song can get.
