Alvvays: Jangle from PEI to the World

The prettiest guitar sound in modern Canadian music comes from a band with a defiantly un-pretty name: Alvvays — five dream-pop perfectionists who trace their lineage to a Prince Edward Island kitchen and arrived, seemingly fully formed, to deliver some of the most exquisitely crafted indie-pop of the past decade. "Archie, Marry Me" proposed to the world. The world said yes.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the island roots

Alvvays coalesced in Toronto but its soul is PEI: singer Molly Rankin comes from one of the island's great musical families (the Rankins — see our Rankins feature), raised in the Celtic-folk tradition of Cape Breton and the eastern shore. That inheritance — melodic directness, harmony instinct, the kitchen-party's unpretentious joy — met the band's other ingredient: the dreamier math of 80s and 90s indie-pop (the Byrds' descendants, the C86 lineage, the shoegaze haze). Rankin's songwriting became the perfect synthesis: melodies that sound found, lyrics about ambivalence and desire delivered with disarming sweetness, and a voice that can arch from conversational to crystalline within a phrase.

Chapter two: the canon years (2014–2022)

The self-titled debut (2014) arrived like a hand-delivered gift: "Archie, Marry Me" — a marriage proposal rendered as three minutes of jangle-pop perfection — became an instant indie standard, and the album's run ("Adult Diversion," "Next of Kin") established the band's signature: every element in its place, nothing wasted. Antisocialites (2017) deepened and darkened — dream-pop with teeth, the critics' favorite and a Juno winner — while Blue Rev (2022) pushed the textures further (more distortion, more dynamics, the same melodic spine) and confirmed what the faithful already knew: Alvvays were one of the great bands on earth, full stop. The live show sealed it: loud, precise, and joyfully committed, the jangle given arena-scale without losing a molecule of intimacy.

Chapter three: the quiet standard-bearers

Alvvays occupy a rare position: critically untouchable, commercially steady, and influential — the band younger indie acts study for proof that craft and cool can coexist. In the lineage of Canadian indie (the Halifax wave → the Montreal explosion → the current moment — see our scene features), they're the perfectionist wing: the ones who made three (and counting) flawless records and never once confused volume for meaning. The island keeps producing them. The jangle keeps traveling.

Why they matter

Alvvays matter because they carry the Canadian jangle tradition — Rankin's folk inheritance, the country's endless melody line — into the present with a level of craft that embarrasses the genre's lo-fi roots. "Archie, Marry Me" will be sung at weddings for fifty years. The rest of the catalog will be studied for a hundred. That's the Alvvays ratio: all pleasure, all precision.

Frequently asked questions

Who are Alvvays?

A Toronto-based (PEI-rooted) dream-pop band fronted by Molly Rankin — one of the most acclaimed indie acts of the 2010s–2020s.

What is Alvvays’ biggest song?

“Archie, Marry Me” (2014) is the signature standard; the albums Antisocialites (2017) and Blue Rev (2022) are the critical landmarks.

Where is Alvvays from?

The band formed in Toronto, but singer Molly Rankin comes from the Rankin family of Prince Edward Island — the Celtic-folk lineage runs deep.
