“Weighty Ghost”: Wintersleep's Spectral Hit, and the Ghost That Followed the Band

Some songs haunt their own authors. “Weighty Ghost” arrived in 2007 as the first single from Wintersleep's Welcome to the Night Sky and immediately did something no one in Halifax's mid-2000s indie boom could fully predict: it became a genuine crossover hit — a spectral, chiming indie-rock song with a singable specter at its center, and the band's calling card ever since.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

The song

“Weighty Ghost” is a ghost story wearing a guitar anthem: a narrator followed by something he can't outrun, set to chiming, echo-laden guitars and Paul Murphy's reedy, urgent voice. The arrangement builds patiently — verses that circle, a chorus that opens like a door — and the production gives the whole thing a nocturnal, filmic quality that set it apart from the blog-rock scrum of 2007. The video, a darkly comic piece of gothic Canadiana, sealed it: MuchMusic put it in rotation, radio followed, and Welcome to the Night Sky — produced with a newfound clarity — carried the band from indie-scene heroes to national names, winning them a Juno for New Group of the Year.

The burden of the ghost

A signature song this size becomes both gift and shadow. Wintersleep kept going — New Inheritors (2010), Hello Hum (2012), and a steady, well-loved career of records and shows — but every set, every interview, every festival slot circles back to “Weighty Ghost.” The band has handled it with grace, treating the song less as an albatross than a handshake: the tune that lets a room full of strangers become a choir. Their later records, including The Great Detachment (2016) with its own radio moments, prove a catalog deeper than its biggest ghost.

The legacy

“Weighty Ghost” endures as one of the defining Canadian indie singles of its decade — the moment the Halifax diaspora (Wintersleep relocated to Toronto as the Maritime boom crested) translated scene energy into a durable national hit. It remains a live ritual: the lights drop, the first chords ring, and thousands of voices take over the chorus. Being haunted, it turns out, is a form of immortality.

Frequently asked questions

Who sings “Weighty Ghost”?

Wintersleep, the Halifax-formed (later Toronto-based) indie rock band fronted by Paul Murphy.

What album is “Weighty Ghost” on?

Welcome to the Night Sky (2007), which won the band a Juno for New Group of the Year.

Is Wintersleep still together?

Yes — the band has continued recording and touring steadily, with “Weighty Ghost” remaining the live centerpiece.
