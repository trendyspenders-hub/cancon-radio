Northern Cree: The Prairie Drum Group the Grammys Keep Nominating

The most Grammy-nominated act you've never heard of might be a powwow drum group from the Canadian prairies. Northern Cree — the Cree singing group from the Treaty 6? — keep it light: the Cree drum group from the prairie communities — have been nominated for the Grammy Award's Indigenous-music categories repeatedly, year after year, carrying powwow music onto the world's biggest awards stage with a consistency the mainstream barely notices and the Indigenous music world celebrates enormously. The story of the drum that keeps reaching the Grammys:

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the powwow circuit

Northern Cree came up through the powwow circuit — the competitive and ceremonial tradition at the heart of Plains Cree culture (see our Indigenous-music feature): drum groups singing at powwows across the prairies and beyond, judged on the precision of the unison singing, the power of the drum, the feeling — the tradition's word for the thing the judges can't quantify. The group built its reputation the circuit's way: year after year of singing, the repertoire deepening, the harmony (the unison's eerie, rolling power — a dozen voices moving as one lung) perfected. The powwow world knew Northern Cree was special long before the Grammys called.

Chapter two: the Grammy years

Then the nominations began: the Grammy's American Roots/Indigenous categories (the category names have shifted over the years — keep it light: the Grammy's Indigenous-music category) recognizing Northern Cree's records repeatedly — the group's appearances on the nomination lists becoming an annual landmark for the powwow world and the Indigenous music community (see our features). Each nomination carried symbolic weight far beyond the category: powwow music — ceremony, competition, community — on the same ballot as the industry's biggest names; the prairie drum in the room where the industry decides what counts. The group attended, performed, represented — carrying the tradition with the dignity the circuit teaches.

Chapter three: what the drum carries

Northern Cree's Grammy run matters because of what the nominations mean: the mainstream industry's periodic acknowledgment that the oldest continuously-practiced music on the continent (see our First Nations 78s feature) belongs on any stage it's offered — and the powwow tradition's own proof that its standard-bearers can stand on the world's stage without changing a note. The group keeps singing, the circuit keeps turning, the nominations keep coming? — keep it light: the nominations keep arriving, and the drum keeps teaching the lesson the whole country's music should remember: the oldest traditions are the livingest ones. Congratulations, relatives. The floor is yours. It always was.

Why they matter

They matter because representation at the industry's top table — earned through tradition, sustained through excellence — changes what the table means. Northern Cree didn't adapt powwow music for the Grammys; the Grammys adapted to powwow music. The drum called the tune. The room listened.

Frequently asked questions

Who are Northern Cree?

A Cree powwow drum group from the Canadian prairies — multiple Grammy nominees in the Indigenous-music categories.

What is powwow music?

The drum-and-voice tradition at the heart of Plains Indigenous culture — competitive, ceremonial, and communal, judged on precision and power.

Why do Northern Cree’s Grammy nominations matter?

They carry powwow music — one of North America’s oldest living traditions — onto the industry’s biggest stage, on the tradition’s own terms.
