Bhangra in British Columbia: A Decades-Long Party

Long before the 905's streaming-era dominance (see our Brampton feature), the West Coast had its own Punjabi music powerhouse. B.C.'s bhangra tradition — rooted in the Surrey-Delta region's huge Punjabi-Canadian community — has been running for four decades: the wedding-band circuit, the club nights, the fusion experiments, and the international stars who made Vancouver a known address in the global Punjabi music world.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the community and the circuit

Punjabi immigration to B.C. built one of the oldest and largest South Asian communities in Canada, and the music followed the community's rituals: bhangra was wedding music, harvest music, community music — the sound of celebration imported from Punjab and kept alive at every Vaisakhi festival, every family event, every community hall dance. The wedding-and-function circuit became the training ground: live bhangra bands honed their craft at hundreds of events a year, and the best of them graduated to recording artists. The infrastructure was community-built decades before the industry noticed — the classic pattern of Canadian diaspora scenes.

Chapter two: the Vancouver stars

The West Coast's scene produced its own internationals — most famously Jazzy B, the "Crown Prince of Bhangra," who built a global career from a Vancouver base, fusing traditional bhangra with hip-hop and R&B and carrying the West Coast sound to Punjabi audiences worldwide. Around the flagship artists, the ecosystem hummed: the club nights that mixed bhangra with West Coast urban music, the video shoots on Surrey's streets, the radio shows, and the cross-pollination with the city's hip-hop and R&B communities. B.C. bhangra was never a niche — it was a scene with its own gravity.

Chapter three: the continuing party

Today, the B.C. tradition continues alongside (and in friendly rivalry with) Ontario's Punjabi powerhouse: the Vaisakhi parade's sound systems, the wedding circuit's ever-evolving bands, the new generation fusing bhangra with trap and pop, and the community institutions that keep the dance itself alive. The four-decade arc is the point: bhangra in B.C. didn't arrive with the streaming era. It built the era's audience, one wedding at a time, for forty years.

Why it matters

B.C. bhangra matters as the West Coast wing of Canada's Punjabi music story — proof that the tradition spans the whole country, built independently in each community that needed it. When the world discovered Punjabi music in the 2020s, Canada had been dancing to it for decades — and B.C. was one of the places that never stopped.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the bhangra scene in Canada?

Both coasts: the Brampton/Toronto 905 (see our feature) and the Surrey-Vancouver region of B.C., each with decades of independent tradition.

Who is the most famous BC bhangra artist?

Jazzy B — the “Crown Prince of Bhangra” — built a global career from Vancouver.

How did bhangra survive in Canada for decades?

Through community institutions: wedding bands, Vaisakhi celebrations, club nights, and radio — the music lived at community events long before the mainstream industry noticed.
