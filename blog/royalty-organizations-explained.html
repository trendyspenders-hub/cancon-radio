Royalty Organizations, Explained Once and For All

Every Canadian musician eventually confronts the same alphabet soup: SOCAN, CMRRA, Re:Sound, MROC, CONNECT — the royalty organizations that collect and distribute the money your music earns when it's performed, played, or reproduced. The system is genuinely confusing — but the basics are learnable in one sitting. Explained, finally:

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READBY THE NUMBERS

The data box: the three rights, the five organizations

The royalty map, simplified - The performance right — when your song is played in public (radio, TV, live venues, streaming): collected by SOCAN for songwriters and publishers, and by Re:Sound for the recording's performers and makers (the neighbouring rights). - The reproduction right — when your song is copied (physical sales, downloads, streams, sync): administered by CMRRA for mechanical royalties, with CONNECT as the newer competitor. - The private-copying levy — the small charge on blank recording media that flows to artists: administered by the CPCC. - The musician's performance fund — MROC collects the neighbouring-rights shares for non-featured (session) musicians. - Who pays whom: radio and venues pay SOCAN and Re:Sound; labels and digital services pay CMRRA/CONNECT; the blank-media levy pays the CPCC; MROC pays the session players. The songwriter gets the song; the performer gets the recording; the session player gets MROC.

What musicians actually need to do

The system's cruel catch: the money is there, but only for the registered. The action items: register with SOCAN when your song exists; register your recordings with Re:Sound and MROC (the session players especially must register); affiliate with CMRRA or CONNECT if you self-publish; and register every song (the title, the splits, the co-writers) — the paperwork is the payday. The musicians who get paid are the ones who registered; the money doesn't find you. You find it.

The debates

The system's controversies: the neighbouring-rights expansion (the decades-long fight to recognize the recording's own performance right), the blank-levy battles, the digital distribution math (the per-stream pennies), and the collectives' own efficiency (the administration fees — the eternal question). The system isn't perfect — but it's the reason the cheques exist at all. Register. Then complain — you've earned the right.

Frequently asked questions

What is SOCAN?

The Society of Composers, Authors and Music Publishers of Canada — collects performance royalties for songwriters and publishers.

What are neighbouring rights?

The performance right in the recording itself (separate from the song) — collected by Re:Sound and MROC for performers and makers.

Do I need to register with the royalty organizations?

Yes — the money only flows to the registered: SOCAN for songwriters, Re:Sound/MROC for performers, CMRRA or CONNECT for the mechanical stream.
