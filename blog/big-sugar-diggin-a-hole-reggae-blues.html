Big Sugar: "Diggin' a Hole" and the Heaviest Groove in Canada

Somewhere between a blues bar, a Kingston sound system, and a southern-rock arena, there's a Venn diagram intersection that only one band has ever fully occupied. Big Sugar — the Toronto institution fronted by Gordie Johnson — built a thirty-year career on a sound no focus group would have approved: reggae's offbeat lilt fused to blues-rock's muscle, played at punishing volume with a two-drummer? — keep it clean: played at punishing volume with thunderous rhythm. Canada adopted them as a national treasure, and the rest of the world has been catching up ever since.

Chapter one: the sound is the thing (1988–1994)

Big Sugar formed in Toronto in the late 80s around Johnson — a Kitchener? — keep it clean: an Ontario-bred guitarist-producer with a feel for both Memphis and Kingston — and a rhythm philosophy borrowed as much from Jamaican dub as from blues shuffles. The early records built the cult: hard-touring, heavy-grooving, with Johnson's slide guitar snaking through riddims that made bar crowds dance like it was a club in Montego Bay. The breakthrough came with "Diggin' a Hole" — the hypnotic single that broke them nationally and announced the formula fully formed: reggae's pulse, rock's weight, and a groove you could set your watch by.

Chapter two: the imperial era (1994–2003)

The mid-90s run made Big Sugar one of Canada's premier live acts. Hemi-Vision (1996) and Heated (1998) delivered the hits ("If I Had My Way," "Nicotina") and the platinum? — keep it clean: and the gold-and-platinum certifications, while the band's live show became legend: Johnson's grinning intensity, the rhythm section's tidal pull, and an audience that danced from the first downbeat. Johnson also became a sought-after producer — his studio fingerprints spreading across Canadian roots-rock — and the band's dub experiments (under alter-ego aliases? — keep it clean: including dub-flavored side projects) showed the reggae half of the equation was no costume but a genuine second language.

Chapter three: endings, reunions, and the long now

Big Sugar wound down in the mid-2000s — Johnson decamped to Texas, chasing the southern-blues part of his sound — and the hiatus only deepened the band's legend. Reunions in the 2010s found an audience grown more devoted in the absence, and the band has settled into the perfect final form of a beloved Canadian institution: festival headliners, occasional records, and the sure knowledge that any given summer night, somewhere in the country, thousands of people are dancing to "Diggin' a Hole" like it's both the first and the thousandth time.

Why they matter

Big Sugar matters because they proved a Canadian band could create a hybrid rather than imitate one — reggae-blues as an original grammar, not a mashup — and sustain it for decades without dilution. In the national conversation about what Canadian music is allowed to sound like, Big Sugar's answer was always the same: everything, all at once, loud.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Big Sugar?

A Canadian reggae-blues-rock band fronted by guitarist Gordie Johnson, formed in Toronto in the late 1980s.

What is Big Sugar’s biggest hit?

“Diggin’ a Hole” is the signature single, with “If I Had My Way” close behind.

Is Big Sugar still together?

The band has cycled through hiatuses and reunions since the mid-2000s and continues to perform and record.
