Shania Twain: The Come On Over Phenomenon

She sang in bars at eight years old to keep the family fed. Twenty-six years later, she owned the best-selling country album in history — and then she lost her voice to Lyme disease and Lyme-related nerve damage, and had to rebuild it from the ground up. Shania Twain's story has at least three comeback arcs, and Canada is woven through all of them.

BY CANCON RADIO4 MIN READA TIMELINE

The timeline

1965–1987Timmins

Eilleen Regina Edwards is born in Windsor, Ontario, and raised in Timmins by a mother who remarried an Ojibwe man, Jerry Twain — the surname Eilleen would adopt and carry into history. Money is short; the singing starts early. By eight she's performing in local bars after last call, passed over the crowd in a spotlight that was part job and part lifeline. In November 1987, her parents are killed in a car crash. She is 22, and she raises her younger siblings while singing demo sessions by day.

1991–1993The discovery

A demo tape reaches Nashville. Mercury Nashville signs her, renames her Shania (from an Ojibwe word, according to legend, meaning "on my way"), and releases Shania Twain (1993). The album barely registers — which sets up one of the great second acts in country music history.

1993Mutt

She meets producer Robert John "Mutt" Lange, the architect of AC/DC's Back in Black and Def Leppard's Hysteria. They marry within the year. Lange hears what Nashville hasn't: Twain isn't a country singer who can cross over — she's a pop architect who happens to speak country fluently. The partnership becomes one of the most productive in recording history, and the marriage becomes tabloid legend before its very public collapse.

1995The Woman in Me

The Lange-Twain machine detonates. The Woman in Me produces five hit singles — "Whose Bed Have Your Boots Been Under?", "Any Man of Mine," "I'm Outta Here!" — and sells by the truckload (eventually diamond in the U.S.). Country radio gets dragged, happily, into a new era of attitude, hooks, and leopard print.

1997Come On Over

The one. Come On Over spends years on the charts and becomes the best-selling country album of all time and one of the best-selling albums by any female artist, ever. The singles are a genre unto themselves: "Man! I Feel Like a Woman!," "That Don't Impress Me Much," "From This Moment On," and "You're Still the One" — a wedding standard that outlived every trend it rode. Twain isn't just dominating country; she's rewriting what a global pop-country crossover looks like.

2002Up! goes global

Twain answers the crossover question with a sledgehammer: Up! ships with three discs — country, pop, and an international version recorded with artists from around the world. It's maximalism as generosity: wherever you live, here's the album in your dialect.

2008–2011The collapse and the diagnosis

The marriage ends publicly — Lange's affair with Twain's close friend, a betrayal she later described as seismic. Around the same period, her voice begins failing. The cause, eventually identified: Lyme disease contracted years earlier, and resulting nerve damage to her vocal cords. For a singer whose entire career rested on clarity and power, the diagnosis reads like a cruel irony.

2012–2018Vegas and the long rebuild

A two-year residency at Caesars Palace (Still the One) proves the demand never left. Behind the scenes, she works with vocal therapists for years — documented with painful honesty — to rebuild her instrument note by note.

2019–presentThe third act

A triumphant Las Vegas return, the 2022 Netflix documentary Not Just a Girl, and Queen of Me (2023) — new music on a rebuilt voice, touring arenas again. The woman from Timmins, twice written off, closes her own loop.

Why Shania mattered beyond the sales

  • She dragged country into the pop age. Hooks first, genre second — the template every post-1997 country-pop act still uses.
  • She controlled the whole machine. Co-writing every song, co-producing the records, shaping the image — Twain was authorship as a business model.
  • She made small-town Canadian girlhood global currency. Timmins wasn't a liability; it was the brand.

Three Twain songs that explain everything

  1. "Any Man of Mine" — the thesis statement: country rhythm, pop hooks, and a woman dictating terms. Nashville had heard attitude before; it hadn't heard it this danceable.
  2. "You're Still the One" — the ballad that let the world sing along at weddings for twenty-five years. Underneath the sweetness: written in the middle of an industry that insisted she wouldn't last.
  3. "That Don't Impress Me Much" — three minutes of shoulder-shrug at male ego, wrapped in the catchiest chorus of 1998. Comedy as empowerment, disguised as a singalong.

Frequently asked questions

What is Shania Twain's best-selling album?

Come On Over (1997), the best-selling country album of all time and one of the best-selling albums by a female artist in history.

What happened to Shania Twain's voice?

Twain was diagnosed with Lyme disease and nerve damage to her vocal cords, which gradually silenced her in the late 2000s. She documented years of vocal rehabilitation in therapy and training, returning to full live performance in the late 2010s.

Where is Shania Twain from?

Timmins, Ontario — where she began performing in bars as a child to help support her family.
