The Wailin' Jennys: Winnipeg's Harmony Institution

Some bands are named for what they do; the Wailin' Jennys — the name a pun on the Waylon Jennings? — keep it light: the name a gentle pun on outlaw-country fame — were named for something better: the wail. Three voices (Nicky Mehta, Ruth Moody, and — across eras — Cara Luft founding, Heather Masse joining) in harmony so precise and warm that the trio became, within a decade, the premier harmony act in North American folk: Juno winners, festival headliners, and the sound a generation associates with the words folk trio.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the café beginnings (2002)

The Jennys formed in Winnipeg in 2002 (see our Winnipeg feature) — a city whose folk tradition runs deep — when the three founding singers (Mehta, Moody, and Luft) were booked for the same café show and discovered, mid-song, what three voices can do when they stop blending and start interlocking. The eponymous debut (2003) captured it: original songs in the Anglo-folk tradition, harmonies that treated the voice as the tradition's lead instrument. The live show built the legend — the three voices around one microphone, the blend so seamless audiences couldn't tell who was singing lead (the point, and the proof).

Chapter two: Firecracker and the breakthrough (2006)

Firecracker (2006) — with Masse replacing Luft — was the breakthrough: the title track and a run of originals and covers that brought the trio Juno recognition (Roots Album honors — keep it clean: Juno-winning acclaim) and the folk circuit's top billing. The records since (Bright Morning Stars, Fifteen — the anniversary album, Hymns from the 49th Parallel? no — keep to: albums through the 2010s and the Fifteen anniversary collection) have refined the formula without altering it: three songwriters, one blend, the repertoire moving between originals and traditionals? — keep it light: between originals and carefully chosen covers. The trio's a cappella performances — the wail, unaccompanied — became the set-piece fans waited for: three voices, no net, perfect.

Chapter three: the institution years

Two decades in, the Jennys are an institution: the folk circuit's gold standard for harmony, the template every young folk trio measures itself against (see our folk features), and the proof that Winnipeg's scene (see our feature) keeps producing world-class musicians the quiet way. Their influence runs through the current trad revival — the harmony-first aesthetic, the communal warmth, the belief that three voices in a room is enough. It is. It always was. The Jennys wail on.

Why they matter

They matter because harmony is folk music's oldest technology — the original technology of togetherness — and the Jennys are its modern masters: proof that three voices, arranged with care and sung with love, can carry a career, a genre, and an audience's heart. The name was a joke. The wail is the real thing.

Frequently asked questions

Who are the Wailin’ Jennys?

A Winnipeg folk trio — Nicky Mehta, Ruth Moody, and Heather Masse (with Cara Luft as a founding member) — among North America’s premier harmony acts.

What is the Wailin’ Jennys’ best album?

Firecracker (2006) is the breakthrough; the debut and the Fifteen anniversary collection anchor the catalog.

Where are the Wailin’ Jennys from?

Winnipeg, Manitoba — formed in 2002 in the city’s deep folk tradition.
