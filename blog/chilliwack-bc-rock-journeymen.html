Chilliwack: Fly at Night, B.C. Rock's Journeymen

The longest-running band in Canadian rock doesn't get its due — mostly because Chilliwack never demanded it. From psychedelic beginnings as the Collectors through the 70s FM era to the 80s singles that finally broke them wide, Chilliwack (named for the Fraser Valley town near where Bill Henderson grew up) was the West Coast's great journeyman band: craftsmanlike, harmonically rich, and quietly essential to the sound of Canadian radio for twenty years.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: from the Collectors (1966–1970)

Henderson's first band, the Collectors, were Vancouver-scene psychedelia — artistic, dramatic, and a bit too far ahead? — keep it clean: artistic and dramatic, with an underground classic ("Early Morning") in their catalog. Reborn as Chilliwack in 1970, the band pivoted toward songcraft: the harmonies stayed, the ambitions got leaner, and the West Coast sound — folk-rock bones, pop instincts, and the era's signature studio polish — began to take shape.

Chapter two: the FM years (1970–1980)

Through the 70s, Chilliwack built the classic CanCon FM catalog: "Lonesome Mary" (1970) announced the melodic gift; "Fly at Night" (1976) became the radio staple — a yearning, harmony-laden anthem that captured the wandering West Coast spirit; the albums stacked gold certifications? — keep it clean: the albums sold steadily and the radio play was constant, even as the band's personnel turned over around Henderson's constant core. The sound was distinctive enough to matter and broad enough to fit everywhere: album rock, pop, and the era's omnivorous FM dial.

Chapter three: the 80s breakthrough and the long tail

Commercial peak arrived with the early 80s: "My Girl (Gone, Gone, Gone)" (1981) — an irresistible, harmony-drenched pop-rock single — and "I Believe" (1981–82) topped the Canadian charts, finally giving the band the mainstream moment twenty years of craft had earned. The later years settled into the journeyman's reward: steady touring, deep catalog recognition, and Henderson's parallel careers in production and mentorship — the band's craft ethic extended into the West Coast scene he helped build.

Why they matter

Chilliwack matters as the connective tissue of B.C. rock: psychedelia to FM craft to 80s pop, all one continuous band, all one songwriter's through-line. They're the band other West Coast musicians cite (see our Vancouver features) — less famous than the giants, but woven through the region's entire musical fabric. "Fly at night" indeed: quiet altitude, long distance.

Frequently asked questions

What were Chilliwack’s biggest hits?

“My Girl (Gone, Gone, Gone)” and “I Believe” (both early 80s) were the chart peaks; “Fly at Night” (1976) is the signature FM-era anthem.

Who is Bill Henderson?

Chilliwack’s founder, singer, guitarist, and principal songwriter — formerly of the Collectors, and a West Coast music institution.

Where is Chilliwack from?

The band emerged from Vancouver’s scene (named for the Fraser Valley city); they’re B.C. rock’s longest-running journeymen.
