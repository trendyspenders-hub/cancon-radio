Ottawa's Surprisingly Rich Music History

Ottawa is the city Canadians fly over — the government town, the postcard Parliament, the place where fun goes to be lobbied. Which makes its music history one of the country's best-kept secrets. The capital has produced chart-toppers, punk pioneers, funk institutions, and one of North America's biggest festivals — all while the rest of the country looked elsewhere.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the unlikely hitmakers

Start with the strangest fact: Paul Anka — Ottawa's own — was one of the biggest teen stars on earth in the late 1950s ("Diana," "Put Your Head on My Shoulder"), writing for Sinatra before he was old enough to vote, and remains one of the most successful Canadian songwriters in history. A generation later, the Five Man Electrical Band gave the world "Signs" (1971), the catchiest protest song ever written by a Canadian band — born, like Anka's career, in the capital's clubs and high school dances. Ottawa's pop pedigree, in other words, predates its reputation by decades.

Chapter two: the punk and underground years

Beneath the government marble, the capital had a rowdy streak. The 70s and 80s brought a scrappy punk and garage scene (the legendary Zaphod Beeblebrox club opened in 1985 and became an institution), and the city incubated acts that punched above the capital's quiet reputation. The scene's defining ethic — nobody's watching, so do what you want — produced music with a distinctive self-reliance that later waves inherited.

Chapter three: the modern capital

The 90s gave Ottawa its biggest mainstream voice since Anka: Alanis Morissette, raised in the city (and sharpened on You Can't Do That on Television) before Jagged Little Pill detonated. The scene around her was real and weird: the Souljazz Orchestra built Ottawa into a funk and Afrobeat hub; K'naan passed through the city's Somali-Canadian community; and the festival machine emerged — Ottawa Bluesfest grew from a blues picnic into one of the largest music festivals in North America, drawing hundreds of thousands to LeBreton Flats every July. Venues like Barrymore's and the National Arts Centre's stages give the city room for both the grassroots and the grand.

Why Ottawa matters

The capital's scene runs on the same fuel as Winnipeg's: isolation from industry, cheap-ish spaces, and a civic personality that prizes competence and community over flash. Its contributions — Anka's songwriting, "Signs'" working-class poetry, Alanis's detonation, Bluesfest's scale — are disproportionate to its profile. Ottawa doesn't need to be cool. It just keeps quietly producing the goods.

Frequently asked questions

What musicians are from Ottawa?

Paul Anka, the Five Man Electrical Band, Alanis Morissette, and the Souljazz Orchestra are the signature names, with a long tail of punk and indie acts from the Zaphod-era scene.

What is Ottawa Bluesfest?

One of North America's largest music festivals, held each July at LeBreton Flats — evolved from a blues festival into a multi-genre mega-event.

Does Ottawa have a music scene?

Yes — a durable one, built on punk-era institutions, the NAC, university and college radio, and festivals, with a do-it-yourself capital character all its own.
