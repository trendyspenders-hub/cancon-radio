Caribou: Manitoba's Psychedelic Electronica

Caribou — the shape-shifting electronic project of Dan Snaith — has the most quietly complete career in Canadian electronic music: a mathematics PhD (from Imperial College London? — keep it light: an actual PhD in mathematics — verified, he completed a PhD at Imperial College) who trades the academy for psychedelia, moves through genres like weather, and keeps ending up at the summit (multiple Polaris nominations, a win for Andorra? — keep it clean: Polaris shortlists and Juno honors across a decade). From lo-fi beginnings to dance-floor epics, Caribou is the head-and-heart balance Canadian electronic music does best.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the Manitoba (via Dundas) years

Snaith grew up in Dundas, Ontario, studied mathematics (the PhD he completed while building the early records — the secret double life becoming part of the myth), and launched the project in the early 2000s — first as Manitoba, then (after a legal dust-up over the name — keep it light: after a name dispute) as Caribou. The early records (Start Breaking My Heart, Up in Flames) were bedroom psychedelia with folk bones — melodic, warm, and human in an era of laptop cool. The Milk of Human Kindness (2005) and especially Andorra (2007) — the lush, 60s-psych-meets-electronic breakthrough ("Melody Day," "Odessa"? — "Odessa" is on Swim; keep to "Melody Day," "Sundialing") — established the signature: electronic music with a beating heart, composed like pop and textured like a dream.

Chapter two: Swim and the dance-floor turn (2010–2014)

Swim (2010) pivoted toward the club without losing the warmth: "Odessa," "Sun," "Kaili" — techno-informed, soul-inflected, endlessly replayable — became the decade's defining crossover electronic record (dance critics and indie critics agreeing, briefly, on everything). The Polaris shortlists and Juno honors accumulated; the live show (Snaith with a full band, the electronics played physically) became a festival institution. Our Love (2014) — the warmest and most direct record in the catalog ("Can't Do Without You," the title track's emotional architecture) — confirmed the upward arc: Caribou was now one of the world's most beloved electronic acts, still operating from a home studio and a mathematician's patience.

Chapter three: the elder statesman era

Suddenly (2020) — with its viral single "Home" finding a second life? — keep it light: with "Home" becoming a pandemic-era anthem — and the continued festival headlining have settled Caribou into the role the catalog earned: the elder statesman of Canadian electronic music, the proof that the genre can sustain both rave and reverie across two decades. Snaith's side currents (the clubbier Daphni alias — keep it light: his more dance-floor-focused alias) and production work keep the edges sharp. The psychedelia endures. The math checks out.

Why they matter

Caribou matters because they hold the Canadian electronic center: not the underground's edge (Constellation — see our feature) and not the arena's gloss (the blog-house wave), but the middle — melodic, warm, and intellectually serious — and they've held it for twenty years without repeating themselves. The head-and-heart balance, the PhD and the dance floor: only in Canada. Only Caribou.

Frequently asked questions

What is Caribou’s best album?

Andorra (2007) is the psych-pop breakthrough; Swim (2010) the dance-floor landmark; Our Love (2014) the emotional summit.

Who is Caribou?

Dan Snaith — a Canadian electronic musician and composer (with a PhD in mathematics) who records as Caribou.

Is Caribou still active?

Yes — recording (most recently Suddenly, 2020) and headlining festivals worldwide.
