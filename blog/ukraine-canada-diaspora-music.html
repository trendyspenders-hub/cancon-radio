Ukraine Meets Canada: Diaspora Music After 2022

Canada holds one of the world's largest Ukrainian diasporas — a community whose musical traditions stretch back to the prairie homesteads of the 1890s. When Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine began in 2022, that century-old heritage met a new wave of arrivals, and the collision produced something moving and new: a Ukrainian-Canadian music scene in the middle of its own renaissance. This is the story of a community singing through history's hardest chapter.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the old tradition

Ukrainian-Canadian music is older than the country that contains it: the homesteaders brought choral traditions, folk instruments (the bandura foremost among them), and a church-music infrastructure that sustained the culture across the prairies for a century. The community's choirs and folk ensembles became Canadian institutions — performing the repertoire at festivals and concert halls, teaching the next generations, keeping the language in song. When the 20th century's upheavals scattered Ukrainians across the globe, Canada's community remained the tradition's great keeper — the place the music was safest.

Chapter two: 2022 and the new wave

The full-scale invasion changed everything. Canada opened its doors to displaced Ukrainians on a scale unseen in generations — musicians among them: classically trained performers, folk artists, rock and pop musicians, and the young generation of Ukraine's own vibrant contemporary scene. The diaspora received them with its institutions ready: the choirs, the cultural centres, the established ensembles. What happened next was the classic Canadian pattern — the new arrivals didn't join the old tradition. They energized it: benefit concerts marshalling the community's full musical weight, collaborations between old-country and long-established diaspora artists, and a new contemporary scene forming where Ukraine's current sounds met Canada's stages.

Chapter three: the music as witness

The music itself carries the moment: benefit performances and fundraising recordings, lullabies-turned-anthems, folk songs taking on new weight, and the contemporary artists documenting displacement and resolve in real time. Canada's music institutions responded in kind — gala concerts, community events, the mainstream industry lending its stages to the cause. Through it all, the bandura and the choir remain the tradition's spine — instruments of memory playing a century of continuity while the newest verse is still being written.

Why it matters

This scene matters because it's Canadian music history happening now, in real time — the latest chapter in the country's oldest story (people arriving with their songs and building something new from them) and one of its most urgent. The Ukrainian-Canadian community has always sung through history. It's singing through this too, and the country is listening.

Frequently asked questions

How big is the Ukrainian-Canadian music community?

Canada holds one of the world’s largest Ukrainian diasporas, with a century-old musical tradition of choirs, folk ensembles, and bandura playing.

What happened to Ukrainian music in Canada after 2022?

The invasion brought a new wave of displaced musicians to Canada, energizing the diaspora tradition — benefit concerts, collaborations, and a new contemporary scene formed from the meeting.

What is the bandura?

A Ukrainian plucked-string folk instrument — the tradition’s signature sound and a symbol of national identity, central to the community’s music in Canada.
