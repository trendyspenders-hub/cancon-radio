Toronto (the Band): "Your Daddy Don't Know" and the New Wave Survivors

Imagine naming your band after your city — the whole city, just "Toronto" — and then living up to it. The band Toronto did exactly that in the early 80s: a female-fronted hard-rock quintet? — keep it clean: a female-fronted rock five-piece that fused the era's new wave sleekness with genuine arena muscle, scored one of the decade's most durable Canadian rock hits, and became one of the great should-have-beens of the country's 80s wave.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the band called Toronto (1979–1982)

Formed in Toronto (where else?) around 1979, the band built its sound on the era's collision: punk's speed, new wave's shine, and arena rock's weight — fronted by Holly Woods, one of the great underrated voices of the Canadian 80s: a powerhouse with new wave cool, equally convincing on the anthems and the ballads. Signed and produced with radio sheen? — keep it clean: signed with a major and produced for maximum radio impact, the band's 1982 album Get It on Credit arrived with everything the era's machine could offer.

Chapter two: the hit (1982)

"Your Daddy Don't Know" became the band's signature — and, decades later, its afterlife: a razor-sharp rocker with a chant-ready title hook, it stormed Canadian radio and remains a CanCon classic-rock staple. The album delivered more (the title track and the ballads showed the band's range), and Toronto seemed positioned for the era's full ride — the look, the sound, the voice, the name. The majors were circling; the next album loomed.

Chapter three: the should-have-been

The classic machinery failure followed: label turbulence and the era's merciless turnover (see our new wave and 80s features) stalled the momentum, and Toronto's window — brief as all early-80s windows were — closed. But the record refused to: "Your Daddy Don't Know" kept circulating through classic-rock programming, compilation appearances, and cover versions, acquiring the permanent afterlife the band's short run never predicted. Woods continued to perform the song for decades, and the band's legacy settled into its final form: one of the era's great one-album statements, fronted by one of its great voices, named for the city it came from and never stopped representing.

Why it matters

Toronto (the band) matters because the 80s Canadian wave was deeper than its survivors: for every Platinum Blonde (see our feature), there was a Toronto — fully equipped, briefly poised, then swept aside by timing. The difference between the canon and the footnote was often one album cycle. "Your Daddy Don't Know" is the proof that the footnotes sometimes write the best songs.

Frequently asked questions

Who sang “Your Daddy Don’t Know”?

Holly Woods, frontwoman of the Toronto band Toronto — one of the great underrated voices of the Canadian 80s.

When did “Your Daddy Don’t Know” come out?

1982, on the album Get It on Credit — a Canadian rock staple ever since.

What happened to the band Toronto?

Label turbulence and the era’s rapid turnover stalled their momentum after the early 80s, though the hit — and Woods’ performances of it — kept the band’s name alive for decades.
