Commander Hadfield's "Space Oddity" and Music's Final Frontier

In May 2013, a Canadian astronaut floating in the International Space Station — on his last day in orbit, after five months commanding the ISS — recorded a music video. It was the first music video filmed in space: Chris Hadfield's zero-gravity cover of David Bowie's "Space Oddity," and for a few days it was the most-discussed piece of music on Earth. The story of the recording that left the planet:

BY CANCON RADIO3 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the most unlikely debut

Hadfield — the Sarnia-raised fighter pilot-engineer-astronaut (Canada's most famous spacewalker — the first Canadian to walk in space, the first Canadian ISS commander) — had spent his five-month mission becoming social media's astronaut: the Twitter photos, the guitar in the station's Cupola module? — keep it light: the guitar sessions floating through the station, the public's falling in love with the guy who made space human. The "Space Oddity" video was the mission's finale: Hadfield and his Earthside son (Evan Hadfield, the video's producer — keep it light: Hadfield, working with his son's production team) conceived the clip as a farewell — the astronaut's ground-control inversion, sung from actual space, the lyrics ("Ground Control to Major Tom") literalized in the most expensive music video ever made? — keep it light: literalized in a production whose location budget no label could match: the International Space Station.

Chapter two: the legal miracle and the launch

The video's existence required two miracles: the recording (performed and filmed aboard the ISS — the guitar floating, the Earth turning in the cupola windows — keep it light: performed and filmed aboard the station, the Earth rotating in the background windows) and the licensing (Bowie's personal blessing, and the unprecedented copyright arrangements for a recording made in orbit — the lawyers' frontier as much as the musician's). The result, released in May 2013, detonated globally: millions of views in days, the press coverage orbiting? — no: the coverage spanning the globe, and the instant canonization of the clip as one of the internet's great cultural moments. Bowie himself called it "possibly the most poignant version of the song ever created" — keep it light: Bowie himself praised it as possibly the song's most poignant version — the ultimate verdict from the source.

Chapter three: the legacy, gravity-free

A decade on, Hadfield's "Space Oddity" endures as the proof that music's oldest dream — the song that goes everywhere — has one frontier left, and we've already reached it: a Canadian voice, a Canadian guitar, an English classic, filmed above the planet that made all three. The video's lesson isn't about space; it's about context (see our sync features): the song everyone knew, sung from a place no one had heard a song from, became new again — the familiar made transcendent by location alone. Music's final frontier, conquered by a Canadian with a guitar and five months of free time. Ground control never stood a chance.

Why it matters

It matters because it's the purest demonstration of what a song can do: cross every border including the atmospheric one, unite a planet in three minutes of shared wonder, and turn an astronaut into an artist and a classic into a first. The first music video in space. The most Canadian flex in the history of the medium: we went up there, and we brought a guitar.

Frequently asked questions

Was “Space Oddity” the first music video in space?

Yes — Chris Hadfield’s 2013 ISS recording was the first music video filmed in space, with David Bowie’s blessing.

Who is Chris Hadfield?

A Canadian astronaut — the first Canadian to walk in space and the first Canadian commander of the International Space Station — and the recording artist behind the space-filmed “Space Oddity.”

How did Hadfield get permission for the Bowie cover?

Bowie personally approved the project, with the rights arrangements negotiated specifically for the orbital recording — a legal as well as musical first.
