Boi-1da: The Architect of Toronto's Rap Sound

If Noah "40" Shebib gave Toronto rap its atmosphere, Boi-1da gave it its anthems. Matthew Samuels — the producer whose tag you'll hear across two decades of hits — made the beats that turned a local scene into a global empire: Drake's first smash, Rihanna's biggest record of the 2010s, Eminem's comeback single. He's the most decorated producer of the Toronto takeover, and the one whose fingerprints are on its biggest moments.

The data box

Boi-1da by the numbers - Born: October 12, 1986, in Toronto — a teenage beatmaker who cut his teeth in local beat battles, winning with FL Studio tracks before he could vote. - The Drake bond: Produced "Best I Ever Had" (2009) — the breakout single that made Drake a star — and has been a core collaborator ever since. - The global wall: Drake's "God's Plan" (2018, 11 weeks at #1), "0 to 100 / The Catch Up"; Rihanna's "Work" (2016, nine weeks at #1); Eminem's "Not Afraid"; Kanye West's "Real Friends"; Jay-Z's "Somewhere in America"? — keep it clean: plus credits across Kanye, Jay-Z, and Nicki Minaj records. - The awards: Multiple Grammys — including for "God's Plan" — plus Juno recognition and producer-of-the-year honors. - The signature: Massive, cinematic drums; ominous melodic loops; beats built like stadium entrances. - The ethos: Still Toronto-based, still mentoring the next generation of Canadian producers.

Chapter one: the battle circuit

Samuels came up the hard, pure way: Toronto's beat-battle circuit, where producers play tracks head-to-head and the room decides. Winning those rooms as a teenager with software-made beats earned him two things — local legend status and the discipline of making drums that hit in a crowd. Both became his trademark. The Boi-1da sound was forged for rooms before it was built for arenas, which is why it still lands like a live event on record.

Chapter two: the anthem factory

"Best I Ever Had" changed Canadian music history — a Toronto beat carrying a Toronto MC to the top of American radio — and Boi-1da's decade since reads like a chart almanac. His partnership with Drake produced "God's Plan" (the 11-week #1 and its record-breaking video), "0 to 100," "Mob Ties," and deep-catalog staples; his work with Rihanna produced "Work," the dancehall-fusion record that ruled 2016; and his range extended far beyond the 6 — Eminem, Kanye, Jay-Z, Nicki Minaj all carry his production. The common denominator: beats engineered for maximum impact without sacrificing musicality — the Toronto sound's stadium wing.

Chapter three: the builder

Unlike producers who chase placements, Boi-1da built infrastructure: a studio hub in Toronto, a generation of protégés (including the wave of young Canadian beatmakers who followed his FL Studio path), and a reputation as the scene's most reliable big-brother figure. He reps Toronto relentlessly — on records, in interviews, in who he works with — and the city reps him back: the architect title isn't honorary. When historians trace how Toronto conquered rap, the map has two signatures. 40 drew the atmosphere. Boi-1da built the monuments.

Frequently asked questions

What is Boi-1da’s biggest hit?

Drake's “God's Plan” (2018) and Rihanna's “Work” (2016) are the landmark #1s; “Best I Ever Had” (2009) is the historically foundational one.

How did Boi-1da get started?

As a teenager in Toronto's beat-battle circuit, winning competitions with tracks made in FL Studio before breaking through with Drake.

Is Boi-1da still working with Drake?

Yes — the partnership continues across Drake's albums into the present era, alongside Boi-1da's work with major international artists.
