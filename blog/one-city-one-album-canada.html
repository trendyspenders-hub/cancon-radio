One City, One Album: The Definitive Record from Every Canadian City

Every Canadian city has one record that is it — the album that captures the place's sound, spirit, and specific weather. Choosing one per city is an arbitrary exercise designed to start arguments (see our debates feature), which is exactly why we did it. The national listening map, city by city:

The city-by-city canon

**St. John's — Great Big Sea, Up (1995)** — The kitchen party at album scale (see our features): trad Newfoundland energy plugged into rock muscle. Runner-up: any Stan Rogers record the island has adopted (see our features).

**Halifax — Sloan, Twice Removed (1994)** — The city's 90s renaissance in one LP (see our features): melodic, self-deprecating, secretly perfect. Runner-up: the Blue Rodeo? no — runner-up: the Marquee-era compilations of memory.

**Charlottetown / PEI — Anne Murray, Snowbird era records** — The island's warmest export (see our features), with the Rankins as the Gaelic wing (see our feature).

Saint John / Fredericton — Stompin' Tom, the New Brunswick years — The patron saint's Maritime phase (see our features) — New Brunswick's own songs, sung by the province's adopted son.

**Québec City — Harmonium, L'Heptade (1976)** — The prog-folk summit (see our feature) — the city's (and the province's) most ambitious record. Runner-up: Beau Dommage (see our feature).

**Montreal — Arcade Fire, Funeral (2004)** — The city's 2000s identity in one album (see our features): the neighborhood as subject, the orchestra as rock band. (The case for The Band is strong; the case for Funeral is stronger.) Runner-up: Leonard Cohen's Songs of Leonard Cohen (see our features).

**Ottawa — Alanis Morissette, Jagged Little Pill (1995)** — The capital's detonation (see our features): raised in the city's suburbs, sharpened on its stages. Runner-up: Paul Anka's early singles (see our Ottawa feature).

Toronto — the unanswerable question — Rush's Moving Pictures (see our features) vs. the Band's catalog vs. Drake's Take Care (see our features): the city is too big for one album, which is the Toronto answer. Pick Moving Pictures and brace for letters.

**Hamilton — Teenage Head, Frantic City (1980)** — The steel city's greaser-punk summit (see our features) — loud, fast, immortal. Runner-up: Arkells' catalog (see our features).

**Winnipeg — the Weakerthans, Reconstruction Site (2003)** — The city as character, written from inside (see our features). Runner-up: the Guess Who's catalog (see our features) — the argument is the city's identity.

The rule of the exercise

Every pick is wrong and every runner-up is right — that's the point (see our debates feature). The map isn't the territory; the map is the argument about the territory, which is how Canadians relate to everything, including music. Your city's pick is wrong. Tell us in the comments. That's the tradition working.

Frequently asked questions

What is the definitive Toronto album?

The honest answer is "unanswerable" — Moving Pictures, the Band's catalog, and Take Care all have claims (see our features); the unanswerability is itself the Toronto answer.

What album defines Montreal?

The consensus leans Arcade Fire's Funeral (2000s identity) with Leonard Cohen's debut as the eternal case (see our features).

Can one album represent a whole city?

No — that's the point of the exercise: the picks start arguments, and the arguments are how a city understands its own sound.
