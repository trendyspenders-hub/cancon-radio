Haviah Mighty: 13th Floor's Feminist Fire

The new center of Canadian rap has a name, and it arrived with a manifesto: Haviah Mighty — the Brampton-raised, Toronto-forged MC whose 13th Floor (2019) won the Polaris Music Prize and announced, in one record, that the country's hip-hop future would be feminist, fearless, and artistically total. The fire had been building for years. The Polaris made it official.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the ascent (2010s)

Mighty came up through Toronto's battle-rap and open-mic circuits (the classic forge — see our features) with a style that refused every available box: technically relentless flows, singing that could pivot to a scream? — keep it light: singing that could turn on a dime into full-throated catharsis, and writing that braided personal narrative with systemic critique — race, gender, the city's contradictions — without ever slipping into lecture. The mixtapes and EPs built the base; the live shows built the legend (an energy the Toronto Star? — no: an energy critics kept calling generational); and the collaborators circled — she was, visibly, the next thing before the institutions caught up.

Chapter two: 13th Floor (2019)

13th Floor — the Polaris winner and the landmark — was the thesis at album length: the title referencing? — keep it light: the title a reference to the hidden floor — the one skipped in elevators, the people skipped in histories — and the record a reclamation: Black womanhood, Canadian identity, the industry itself, examined with a fury and a craft that made resistance sing. The production (eclectic, hard-hitting, unclassifiable) matched the writing's ambition; the live show became a must-see (see our live-bands feature); and the Polaris victory — hip-hop's continued dominance of Canada's most prestigious music prize (see our Polaris feature) — announced the arrival in terms the whole industry had to acknowledge. Center of gravity, shifted.

Chapter three: the fire continues

The years since have confirmed rather than cooled: continued recording and touring, collaborations across the scene's generations, and the role — increasingly public — of advocate and door-opener (the mentorship and visibility work for women in Canadian rap, treated as mission rather than side project). Haviah Mighty represents the culture's present and its direction: technically supreme, politically total, artistically unafraid. The 13th floor was always there. She built the elevator.

Why she matters

She matters because the Canadian rap canon was overdue its reckoning with who gets to hold the microphone — and Mighty didn't wait for permission, taking the stage and the prize and making the case in the music's own terms. The fire is the point. The floor is open. Climb.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Haviah Mighty?

A Brampton/Toronto MC — Polaris Prize winner (2019) for 13th Floor — among the leaders of Canadian hip-hop’s current generation.

What is 13th Floor?

Haviah Mighty’s 2019 Polaris-winning album — the landmark record of her arrival.

What is Haviah Mighty known for?

Technically relentless rapping, feminist and systemic critique, generational live energy, and door-opening advocacy for women in Canadian rap.
