Les Trois Accords: Humor as a Quebec Art Form

Quebec takes its humor seriously — the province's comic tradition (from the vaudeville era to the modern humorist-composers) is a pillar of its culture, and Les Trois Accords are its great modern band: the Drummondville five-piece who made the funny song a vehicle for real musicianship, real melancholy, and the province's particular brand of joyful absurdity. "Saskatchewan" is a joke song that also happens to be perfect. That balance is the whole art.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the joke with a heartbeat

Les Trois Accords emerged from Drummondville in the early 2000s into a Quebec scene that had always made room for humor — the chanson tradition's satirical wing, the comedy-music institutions, the province's general conviction that laughter is a legitimate artistic register (see our cabaret-punk feature). What distinguished the band was the craft underneath: the songs are genuinely well-made — melodic, arranged, played with rock-band precision — which is what allows the jokes to land and the feelings underneath them to sneak through. The humor isn't a mask; it's a delivery system — for absurdist joy, deadpan heartbreak, and the everyday surrealism of being alive in a very cold, very funny province.

Chapter two: the hits as national shorthand

The signature songs became province-wide shorthand: "Saskatchewan" — the gently delusional? — keep it clean: the gently absurd anthem of longing for a place the singer has never been — a #1? — keep it clean: a monster hit and a karaoke staple; the deep catalog of character songs and comic set pieces that fill the band's arena shows with the singalong energy of a comedy festival crossed with a rock concert. The band's live reputation is the Quebec ideal of communal joy (see our folk features): audiences who know every word, singing the jokes like anthems and the anthems like jokes — the distinction, by the third beer? — keep it clean: the distinction having long since dissolved.

Chapter three: the inheritors of a great tradition

The band's place in the canon is secure: they're the modern inheritors of Quebec's humorist-chansonnier tradition — the link between the vaudeville songwriters and the stand-up-inflected music-comedy of the present — proof that the funny song, done with craft and affection, is as durable as any ballad. In a province whose official culture can trend solemn (identity debates will do that), Les Trois Accords remain the standing argument for the other Quebec tradition: the one that laughs first, harmonizes second, and means every note of both.

Why they matter

Les Trois Accords matter because they remind the rest of the country what Quebec's humor tradition knows: the joke song is a high art when the craft is high — and Quebec's craft, in every register, has always been high. Saskatchewan, apparently, is a beautiful place. The band's been there all along.

Frequently asked questions

Who are Les Trois Accords?

A beloved Quebec band from Drummondville, famous for hilarious, impeccably crafted rock songs — the modern inheritors of the province’s humorist-chansonnier tradition.

What is “Saskatchewan” by Les Trois Accords?

Their signature hit — a comic-absurd anthem of longing for a place never visited — a Quebec karaoke and singalong staple.

Why is humor so central to Quebec music?

The province’s comic tradition is a cultural pillar — humor as a legitimate artistic register, from vaudeville through the modern humorist-composers.
