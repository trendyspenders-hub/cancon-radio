The Greatest Canadian Live Performances Ever Captured

The live album (see our live-albums feature) is Canadian music's great argument — proof that the country's bands were better in the room than on the record. These are the performances history kept the tape of, ranked by how definitively they argued the case:

BY CANCON RADIO3 MIN READA STORY
  1. Jazz at Massey Hall (May 15, 1953) — Parker, Gillespie, Powell, Mingus, Roach: the "greatest jazz concert ever," and it happened in Toronto (see our Massey Hall feature). The five titans, one night, one room, preserved on a consumer tape recorder because the professionals assumed nobody would care. They cared.
  2. **Neil Young, *Live at Massey Hall 1971*** — the solo acoustic night between After the Gold Rush and Harvest, recorded and shelved for 36 years (see our Young features). When it finally surfaced in 2007, it was immediately canonized: the greatest live document of the greatest Canadian songwriter at the exact moment he became himself.
  3. **The Tragically Hip, Live Between Us (1997)** — the Fully Completely-era arena show that proved the Hip's real instrument was the crowd (see our Hip features). Twenty thousand Canadians singing every word of "Nautical Disaster" back at the stage — the country, audible.
  4. **The Band, The Last Waltz (1976)** — technically San Francisco, but four-fifths Canadian and therefore ineligible to be excluded: the most famous farewell concert ever filmed, with Dylan, Young, Mitchell, and Van Morrison as witnesses (see our Band feature).
  5. **Rush, Exit... Stage Left (1981)** — the trio's musicianship proved on tape (see our Rush features). The side-long "YYZ" alone justifies the existence of the live-album format.
  6. **Leonard Cohen, Live in London (2009)** — the elder Cohen, post-financial-ruin, touring with the graciousness of a man who'd finally made peace with the songs (see our Cohen features). Three hours a night, in his seventies, every lyric landed (see our features).
  7. **k.d. lang, the Ingénue tour** — no official album, but the bootleg canon agrees: lang in her torch-prime, commanding rooms with a voice that made arenas feel like kitchens (see our lang feature).
  8. Godspeed You! Black Emperor, live anywhere — the Montreal collective's shows (see our Constellation feature) functioned less as concerts than as weather systems; the captured recordings (see our features) preserve the dread and the communion in equal measure.
  9. **Feist, *Look at What the Light Did Now*** — the The Reminder-era tour documented with the same care as the records (see our Feist feature): proof that "quiet" performers (see our live-bands feature) could hold festival fields.
  10. The Hip's final show (Kingston, August 20, 2016) — eleven million Canadians watching one man sing himself home (see our Hip final-tour feature). It wasn't a concert. It was a national ceremony — and the broadcast is the performance history kept.

The live-Canadian thesis

The pattern (see our live-bands feature): Canadian bands, forged in bar rooms and community halls (see our venue features), treat the live show as the point — the record as the advertisement. That's why the captured performances (see our features) so often surpass the studio originals: the country's best music was always happening, in rooms, in real time. The tape (see our one-take feature) just caught it. Thank the tapers. The rooms (see our venues features) live on in the recordings — even where the rooms themselves are gone.

Frequently asked questions

What is the greatest live Canadian album?

Live at Massey Hall 1971 (Young) and Live Between Us (the Hip) top most lists, with Exit... Stage Left (Rush) close behind.

Is the Last Waltz a Canadian performance?

Four-fifths of the Band was Canadian, and the guest list was half Canadian — but it happened in San Francisco. We claim it anyway (see our Band feature).

Where can I hear the Hip’s final show?

The CBC broadcast is the document — one of the most-watched concerts in Canadian television history (see our final-tour feature).
