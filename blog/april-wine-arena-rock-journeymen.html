April Wine: Canada's Arena Rock Journeymen

April Wine never had a mythology. What they had was reliability — five decades of it, from Halifax school gyms to the world's arenas, through every mutation of rock fashion, led by one of the country's most quietly gifted writer-frontmen. They're the journeymen of Canadian rock: the band that was always there, always good, and — as the recent loss of leader Myles Goodwyn reminded the country — far more important than the modest mythology ever let on.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the Halifax beginnings (1969)

April Wine formed in Halifax in 1969 — teenagers chasing the new rock and roll — and did what ambitious Maritime bands did: moved to Montreal, where the industry was. The early records built them as a solid Canadian album act, and "You Could Have Been a Lady" (1972) gave them a genuine hit — a cover choice? — no, an original with a swaggering groove — that announced the band's twin strengths: Goodwyn's writing and the band's unshowy, airtight musicianship.

Chapter two: the arena years (1979–1985)

The imperial run arrived at the turn of the 80s. Harder... Faster (1979) delivered "I Like to Rock" — the mission statement as hit single, a brawny, double-guitar anthem that became the band's eternal calling card (and arena-shout staple). Then The Nature of the Beast (1981) broke them in the US: "Just Between You and Me" — a power ballad with actual craft — became their biggest international record, with MTV-era rotation and American radio saturation. Through the period, April Wine toured relentlessly and stacked platinum at home: the working band's reward for being good at everything — the crunch of "I Like to Rock," the sweetness of "Just Between You and Me," the AOR sheen of "Sign of the Gypsy Queen."

Chapter three: the long road

April Wine never stopped. Through the 80s' changing tides, the 90s' format collapses, and the reunion-era festival circuit, the band toured on — Goodwyn's voice holding, the catalog deepening, the Maritime work ethic undimmed (see our hardest-working-bands feature). Goodwyn's death in 2023 closed the book on one of the great Canadian rock careers — writer, singer, producer, and the steady hand behind one of the country's most durable catalogs. The tributes confirmed what the fans had known all along: April Wine was never the flashiest band in the country. They were the reliable one — and reliability, sustained for fifty years, is its own kind of legendary.

Why they matter

April Wine matters because they model the career rather than the moment: the Halifax-to-Montreal pipeline (see our regional features), the arena-rock craft (see our 80s features), and the workhorse touring tradition that made Canadian rock a self-sustaining industry. "I Like to Rock" says it plainly — and for five decades, they did.

Frequently asked questions

Who was April Wine’s leader?

Myles Goodwyn — singer, guitarist, principal songwriter, and producer, who led the band from its 1969 Halifax formation until his death in 2023.

What were April Wine’s biggest hits?

“I Like to Rock” (1979) is the signature anthem; “Just Between You and Me” (1981) was their biggest international hit.

Is April Wine still together?

The band’s classic era ended with Myles Goodwyn’s passing in 2023; the catalog and legacy continue as Canadian rock canon.
