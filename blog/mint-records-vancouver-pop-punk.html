Mint Records: Vancouver's Pop-Punk Powerhouse

Some labels are built in boardrooms. Mint Records was built by friends in a van. Founded in Vancouver in 1991 — in the thick of the era when the city's music scene was exploding — Mint became the West Coast's happiest, scrappiest indie: a pop-punk and garage powerhouse that treated DIY as a joy rather than a posture. Its catalog is a secret history of Canadian indie, with detonations (the New Pornographers) hiding inside a long run of beloved underdogs.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the van years (1991–1995)

Mint was founded by Randy Iwata and Bill Baker, the latter the irrepressible frontman of the Smugglers — Vancouver's garage-rock evangelists, whose relentless touring made them the connective tissue of a whole coast scene. The label's early releases came from that world: the Smugglers themselves, the all-women punk trio cub (whose "Magic 8 Ball" and Box of Hair era made them international K Records? — keep it clean: made them cult favorites across the Pacific Northwest), and the Evaporators — the band fronted by a certain Nardwuar the Human Serviette, whose manic energy would eventually make him Canada's most beloved interviewer while Mint kept releasing his records. The ethic was total: hand-drawn covers, friends on the payroll, shows in legion halls, pop songs played at punk speed.

Chapter two: the detonation (2000)

Mint's place in history was secured by an album it released almost casually: **the New Pornographers' *Mass Romantic*** (2000). The Vancouver power-pop supergroup — A.C. Newman, Dan Bejar, Neko Case, and friends — made a debut so instantly canonical that critics reached for decade-best language on arrival. It minted? — it minted is too cute; it launched three major careers (Newman, Bejar's Destroyer, Case's solo ascent) and reintroduced Canadian power pop to the world. That it happened on a Vancouver indie run out of friendship rather than strategy remains one of the great arguments for the label way of doing things.

Chapter three: the long middle and the modern wave

The 2000s deepened the catalog: The Organ's Joy Division-tinged darkwave found a devoted audience; Bella, The Buttless Chaps? — keep it clean: and a run of West Coast indie-pop kept the release schedule lively. In the 2010s, Mint pivoted to the heavy end with The Pack A.D. — the two-woman garage-blues juggernaut who became the label's modern flagship, touring ferociously and releasing a string of acclaimed records. Through it all, the label stayed exactly what it was: independent, musician-run, and visibly delighted to be doing it.

Why Mint matters

Mint's three-decade run is the West Coast counterweight to the Toronto-Montreal axis — proof that Vancouver's indie tradition was always its own thing: scrappier, sunnier, more likely to put a band in a van than a boardroom. And its greatest legacy might be the attitude: the idea that a label can be run like a clubhouse and still change music history, one power-pop detonation at a time.

Frequently asked questions

Who started Mint Records?

Randy Iwata and the Smugglers' Bill Baker, in Vancouver in 1991.

What was Mint Records’ biggest release?

The New Pornographers' Mass Romantic (2000) — one of the most acclaimed Canadian debuts of its era, released on Mint.

Is Mint Records still active?

Yes — still independent and Vancouver-based, with The Pack A.D. among its flagship acts of recent years.
