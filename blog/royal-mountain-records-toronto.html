Royal Mountain Records: Toronto's New Guard

By the mid-2010s, Canadian indie needed new blood — a label for the post-blog, post-MuchMusic generation, built by someone who'd actually been through the machine. Enter Menno Versteeg, frontman of the beloved Toronto party-rock band Hollerado, who in 2015 turned his band's DIY infrastructure into Royal Mountain Records. Within five years, Royal Mountain had the most talked-about roster in the country: PUP, Alvvays, Orville Peck, and a wave of young acts who made "Royal Mountain band" its own sound-adjacent identity.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the musician's label

Versteeg's founding insight was practical: Hollerado had spent years learning how to release records, book tours, and survive on van gas — why not point that infrastructure at bands he loved? The label's earliest signings came from Toronto's all-ages and garage scenes, and the ethos was explicitly artist-first: fair deals, real tour support, and a founder who answered his own email because he'd been the guy sending those emails his whole adult life. Royal Mountain also quickly distinguished itself with its commitment to mental health supports for artists — a stance that read as values-driven and, in an industry with a well-documented wellness crisis, quietly radical.

Chapter two: the roster detonates

The label's reputation went national and then global on the backs of three very different bands. PUP — the Toronto four-piece whose self-titled debut announced a new standard for anthemic, self-deprecating punk — became the label's flagship through sheer force of touring and songwriting (The Dream Is Over, Morbid Stuff, The Unraveling of PUPTHEBAND: a run of records that turned Royal Mountain into a punk destination). Alvvays — the jangle-pop perfectionists — released their early work with the label's support on the way to international critical coronation. And Orville Peck, the masked country crooner, arrived via Royal Mountain to become one of the decade's most distinctive debuts, with Pony earning global attention and Polaris recognition. Add Calpurnia, Shad? — keep it clean: add Calpurnia and a steady stream of young signings, and by 2020 Royal Mountain was the most exciting new Canadian label in years.

Chapter three: the model going forward

Royal Mountain's growth tracked the industry's shift from albums to ecosystems: aggressive sync placement, vinyl culture, direct-to-fan, and heavy touring. The label expanded its team and its international distribution while keeping the founding posture — bands first, deals short and fair, Toronto forever. In a landscape where Canadian acts increasingly self-release or sign globally on day one, Royal Mountain makes the case that a domestic label can still be the better deal: local muscle, global ambition, and a boss who's played your stage.

The legacy

Royal Mountain is the proof that Canada's label tradition keeps renewing itself — Nettwerk's patience, Arts & Crafts' community, Mint's joy, and now Versteeg's artist-first hustle, pointed at the next generation. The roster speaks for itself, and loudly.

Frequently asked questions

Who founded Royal Mountain Records?

Menno Versteeg, frontman of Hollerado, in Toronto in 2015.

What bands are on Royal Mountain Records?

PUP, Alvvays, Orville Peck, Calpurnia, and a rotating crop of young Canadian indie and punk acts.

Is Royal Mountain Records independent?

Yes — independently owned, Toronto-based, and known for artist-friendly deals and mental health supports.
