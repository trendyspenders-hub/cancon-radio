Fact-Checking Canadian Music's Biggest Myths

The myth (see our legends feature) is (see our features) music history's unpaid intern (see our features): everywhere (see our features), helpful (see our features), often wrong (see our features). The biggest Canadian myths (see our features), fact-checked (see our features):

The verdicts

Myth: "American Woman" was written as a protest of the Vietnam War draft. Verdict: MISLEADING. The song began as an onstage improvisation (see our feature) aimed at a heckler; the "war machines" imagery gave it a political life its authors later described ambivalently. The protest reading stuck because it fit — the myth improved the song's legend, if not its history.

Myth: Lightfoot wrote "The Wreck of the Edmund Fitzgerald" in one sitting after reading a news story. Verdict: PARTIALLY TRUE. He clipped the article and wrote carefully over time (see our feature) — the research-driven composition is real; the one-sitting romance is not. The lyric changes decades later (see our feature) are fully documented, however — the rare myth that's true.

Myth: The Tragically Hip's final setlist was predetermined before the tour. Verdict: UNPROVEN. The setlists varied nightly (see our final-tour feature) with common anchors; the "scripted goodbye" narrative is retrospective tidying. What is true: the final song was always "Ahead by a Century" — the one prophecy the band chose to keep.

Myth: Neil Young became an American citizen to vote against Trump. Verdict: TRUE. Young completed his naturalization in 2020 and said so explicitly (see our legends feature) — one of the rare myths with a first-person source.

Myth: Drake discovered The Weeknd. Verdict: OVERSTATED. Drake amplified him enormously (see our Trilogy feature), but the Weeknd's tapes were already circulating; the discovery myth understates the artist's own groundwork and overstates the cosign. Both things are true: the cosign mattered; the myth inflates it.

Myth: Celine Dion recorded "My Heart Will Go On" in one take. Verdict: CONTESTED. Dion has described the session as swift and the vocal as early-in-the-session (see our feature); the "one take" is a show-business compression. The hesitation about recording it at all is the documented truth.

Frequently asked questions

Are music myths harmful?

Mildly — they erase craft, but they keep stories alive. The fact-check restores the humans without killing the legends.
