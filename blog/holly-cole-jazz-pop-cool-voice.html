Holly Cole: Jazz-Pop's Cool Canadian Voice

In the early 90s, when "jazz" still conjured cocktail lounges for most of Canada, Holly Cole did something radical: she treated the Great American Songbook with post-punk attitude. The result — deadpan wit, noir cool, impeccable musicianship — created a distinctly Canadian version of jazz-pop and made her one of the most distinctive voices of the decade.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the cool arrives (1990–1993)

Cole emerged from Toronto's club scene with a trio sound built on space and irony: standards and pop songs delivered with a raised eyebrow, behind-the-beat phrasing, and a rhythm section that swung like a secret. The breakthrough records — Girl Talk (1990) and the platinum Don't Smoke in Bed (1993), with its beguiling cover of "I Can See Clearly Now" — made her an international jazz-pop star and proved the formula: jazz credibility, pop accessibility, and a persona entirely her own. She wasn't singing the standards so much as winking at them — and audiences adored the game.

Chapter two: the Waits turn (1995)

The audacious masterstroke was Temptation (1995): an entire album of Tom Waits songs recast as smoky jazz-noir — her sensibility and his songbook revealing each other as natural allies. Critics swooned; the album became a cult classic and reintroduced Waits' writing to a whole audience. It remains one of the great tribute albums by a Canadian artist — not imitation, but translation.

Chapter three: the long, stylish career

Cole never chased trends; the trends kept finding her. Through records like Dark Dear Heart (1997), Shade (2003), and steady international touring, she refined the signature: cool but never cold, ironic but deeply musical, equally at home in a Tokyo jazz club or a European festival stage. She became Canada's premier jazz-pop export — the artist audiences in a dozen countries associated with the country's particular strain of sophisticated song.

Why she matters

Holly Cole matters because she expanded what Canadian jazz-pop could be: not revivalism, not crooning nostalgia, but smart modern art with a pulse. Before the retro-cocktail wave, before the standards revival became an industry, Cole had already staked the claim: the songbook belongs to whoever brings the most personality to it. She brought plenty.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Holly Cole?

A Canadian jazz-pop singer from Toronto, internationally acclaimed since the early 90s for her cool, ironic interpretation of standards and pop songs.

What is Holly Cole’s most famous album?

Temptation (1995), her acclaimed album of Tom Waits songs, is the cult classic; Don’t Smoke in Bed (1993) was the commercial breakthrough.

What style does Holly Cole sing?

Jazz-pop — Great American Songbook material and contemporary songs delivered with a signature blend of swing, space, and dry wit.
