Late-Night Load-Outs: The Venue Workers' Stories

The audience leaves at 11:30. The band leaves at 12:30. The venue crew — the stagehands, the sound engineer, the door staff, the cleaner, the manager locking up — works until 3, 4, sometimes 5 a.m., packing the night back into cases and returning the room to being a room. Every show you've ever loved ended, for someone, with a load-out in an empty building. Their stories, told at last:

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the invisible shift

The venue worker's evening runs opposite to the audience's: the doors open on their ninth hour; the headliner's first chord is their fifteenth; the encore is their cue to start breaking down the monitors. The work is physical (the amp that weighs what a piano weighs), the hours are anti-social (the 4 a.m. drive home through the empty city), and the pay is (mostly) modest — and the crew does it anyway, show after show, because the work is adjacent to joy: you don't play the show, but you hold the show — the band's impossible night made possible by your hands.

Chapter two: the Canadians who hold the rooms

Canada's venue crews carry the national multiplier: the winter load-ins (the gear through the snow), the distance tours that arrive broken and leave fixed (the crew as pit crew), the small-market rooms where one person is the crew (the sound engineer doubling as stagehand, the manager doubling as door), and the union halls and the clubs and the arenas alike — the rooms hold because the crews hold them. Ask any touring band about the best venues in the country and they'll name rooms; ask them why and they'll describe the crew.

Chapter three: the hour the building belongs to them

There's a moment, the veterans say, when the load-out ends and the empty room is theirs — the house lights half-up, the silence enormous, the stage they'd built for someone else standing empty and perfect — the hour when the building belongs to the people who hold it. Some play the empty stage (a chord, a joke — the crew's private encore). Some just sit. Then the lights go off, the door locks, and tomorrow it begins again. The audience never sees the load-out. The show was never possible without it.

Frequently asked questions

What is a load-out?

The post-show teardown: gear packed, cases loaded, the venue returned to an empty room — typically hours after the audience leaves.

Who works venue crews?

Stagehands, sound and lighting techs, door staff, cleaners, and managers — the invisible labor every show depends on.

Why are venue crews important to Canadian music?

Every room and every tour functions because of them — the crew is the infrastructure the mythology forgets.
