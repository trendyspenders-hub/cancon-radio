Wilf Carter: Canada's Original Singing Cowboy

Long before Nashville had a pipeline to Canada, a farm kid from Port Hilford, Nova Scotia, heard a yodel on a passing Victrola? — keep it clean: heard a yodelling record as a boy and spent the rest of his life turning that sound into a career, a genre, and a national tradition. Wilf Carter — known to millions as Montana Slim — is where Canadian country music begins. Everyone who came after, from Hank Snow to Corb Lund, rides a trail he cut.

Chapter one: the Maritimes to the range (1904–1930s)

Born in 1904 in rural Nova Scotia, Carter absorbed the musical stew of the Maritimes — Celtic ballads, gospel, parlor songs — and, fatefully, the Swiss-German yodel he heard on early records and radio. Drawn west by the romance of the ranching frontier, he worked as a cowboy in Alberta and began performing the songs he wrote about the life: real ranch work, horses, weather, loneliness. In 1933, in a Montreal recording studio, he cut the sides that made history — among them songs of the North-West Mounted Police? — keep it clean: among them the yodelling, cowboy ballads that became the template. Canada's first genuine country star had arrived, decades before the industry had a word for what he was.

Chapter two: Montana Slim and the broadcasting years

Radio made Carter continental. Settling into a decades-long broadcasting career — most famously under the name Montana Slim, a handle American networks preferred? — keep it clean: a handle that opened doors on both sides of the border — he became one of the most-heard voices in North America, his yodel instantly recognizable, his songs (hundreds of them) recording the West in melody. He was a fixture on Canadian radio and a star of the barn-dance era, and he kept recording and performing for an astonishing span — his career stretching from the Depression into the 1990s, with a comeback surge late in life that introduced him to yet another generation.

Chapter three: the foundation

Carter's importance outruns his chart history, because he pre-charted the genre: he's the reason Canada has a cowboy songbook at all. His subjects — the ranch, the horse, the trail, the Mountie, the winter — created the vocabulary every western-Canadian songwriter after him would draw on. The honors accumulated: halls of fame, lifetime recognitions, and a permanent place in the story told about Canadian music — the first page of the country chapter.

Why he matters

Every tradition needs a first voice, and Wilf Carter was Canada's: the singing cowboy who proved this country could produce its own country music — rooted not in imitation of the American South but in the actual West, the actual Maritimes, the actual work. Yodel once, from a Nova Scotia farm, and start an industry. It's the most Canadian origin story in the genre.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Wilf Carter?

A pioneering Canadian country singer-songwriter from Nova Scotia — known on the airwaves as Montana Slim — who began recording in the 1930s and helped found Canada’s cowboy music tradition.

What was Wilf Carter’s stage name?

Montana Slim — the broadcasting name he used for decades, especially on radio.

When did Wilf Carter record?

His first recordings date to 1933 in Montreal; he recorded and performed into the 1990s, one of the longest careers in Canadian music.
