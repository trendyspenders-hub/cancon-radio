The Ultimate Canadian Music Bracket: 64 Artists, Head-to-Head

Every debate this website hosts — the songbook, the canon, the underrated, the overrated (see our features) — converges on one question: who's the greatest? We can't answer it (nobody can — see our debates feature), but we can tournament it: 64 artists, four regions, head-to-head, seeded by our editorial judgment and decided by you. The bracket, revealed:

BY CANCON RADIO3 MIN READA STORY

The regions and seeds

REGION 1 — THE FOUNDERS (pre-1970) — the artists who built the grammar:

  1. Leonard Cohen vs 16. Wilf Carter — the poet vs the cowboy: philosophy vs foundation
  2. Joni Mitchell vs 15. The Diamonds — two ends of the early export trade
  3. Neil Young vs 14. Oscar Peterson — guitar vs piano, both untouchable
  4. Gordon Lightfoot vs 13. The Band — the laureate vs the collective; yes, the Band's eligible here
  5. The Guess Who vs 12. Ian & Sylvia — Winnipeg rock vs the folk bridge
  6. Buffy Sainte-Marie vs 11. Hank Snow — the activist vs the Opry legend
  7. Anne Murray vs 10. Stompin' Tom — the warmest voice vs the stompiest boot
  8. Paul Anka vs 9. The Crew-Cuts — Ottawa's teen idol vs Toronto's chart heist (see our features)

REGION 2 — THE CANCON ERA (1970–1995) — the quota-built golden age:

  1. The Tragically Hip vs 16. Loverboy — Kingston's poets vs the red-leather everymen
  2. Rush vs 15. Men Without Hats — prog's peak vs the maypole (see our features)
  3. Bryan Adams vs 14. Glass Tiger — the export machine vs the 80s gloss
  4. Céline Dion vs 13. Alannah Myles — Quebec's empire vs Elvis in black velvet
  5. Shania Twain vs 12. Tom Cochrane — the phenomenon vs the highway
  6. Alanis Morissette vs 11. k.d. lang — the pill vs the torch (see our features)
  7. Sarah McLachlan vs 10. Blue Rodeo — Lilith vs the Queen Street institution
  8. Barenaked Ladies vs 9. Jann Arden — comic craft vs heartbreak craft

REGION 3 — THE INDIE & ALT WAVE (1995–2015) — the scenes go global:

  1. Arcade Fire vs 16. Mother Mother — the orchestra vs the harmony machine
  2. Drake vs 15. Feist — the takeover vs the whisper
  3. The Weeknd vs 14. Broken Social Scene — the nocturne vs the collective (see our features)
  4. Nickelback vs 13. Metric — the reappraisal case vs the survivors (see our features)
  5. Avril Lavigne vs 12. Billy Talent — the blueprint vs the arena punks (see our features)
  6. Tegan and Sara vs 11. Death From Above 1979 — the twins vs the noise duo (see our features)
  7. The New Pornographers vs 10. Stars — power pop vs wounded pop (see our features)
  8. Alvvays vs 9. Caribou — the jangle vs the head-and-heart (see our features)

REGION 4 — THE NEW SCHOOL (2015–present) — the streaming generation:

  1. Justin Bieber vs 16. Jessie Reyez — the phenomenon vs the testimony
  2. Shawn Mendes vs 15. Daniel Caesar — the Vine kid vs the church voice (see our features)
  3. Tate McRae vs 14. The Kid Laroi — the dancer vs the crossover (see our features)
  4. Carly Rae Jepsen vs 13. Kaytranada — the cult pop vs the glow (see our features)
  5. Grimes vs 12. Purity Ring — the universe vs the shimmer (see our features)
  6. Haviah Mighty vs 11. Backxwash — the Polaris generation's civil war (see our features)
  7. JP Saxe vs 10. Alessia Cara — the duet vs the anti-party anthem (see our features)
  8. K'naan vs 9. Nelly Furtado — the flag vs the shape-shifter (see our features)

How to play

Print the bracket (or argue from your couch). First-round rule: one song's evidence allowed per matchup — the single track that best argues each artist's case, nothing else. This produces delightful chaos (seeded giants falling to one perfect record — see our song-story features for ammunition) and honest results (the bracket measures peak, not career — see our debates feature for why that's the right metric). Fill it in. Fight about it. The bracket is the conversation, and the conversation is the country's favorite sport. Play ball. Politely.

Frequently asked questions

How was the Canadian music bracket seeded?

By editorial judgment across four era-regions — founders, CanCon era, indie/alt wave, and new school — seeded for maximum first-round chaos.

What are the rules?

One song per matchup as evidence: the single track that best argues each artist's case. The bracket measures peak, not career.

Who would win a Canadian music bracket?

That's the point — fill it in and fight about it (see our debates feature). The arguing is the national sport.
