“Your Love”: Lime’s Hi-NRG Anthem, and the Montreal Studio That Built It

In 1985, a song with a bubbling synth bassline, a four-on-the-floor kick, and a vocal pitched somewhere between passion and circuitry climbed dance charts across Europe. “Your Love” by Lime was the work of two Montrealers — Denis and Denyse LePage — recording in a home studio with machines they'd essentially taught themselves. It's one of the great hidden-in-plain-sight Canadian dance records.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

The duo and the sound

Lime was the LePage partnership in every sense: husband and wife writing, producing, and performing together in their Montreal studio, part of the small but fierce Canadian hi-NRG scene that paralleled Italo disco across the Atlantic. Where their Italian contemporaries were glossy and cinematic, Lime had a leaner, loop-driven approach — sequenced basslines, stabbing synths, and choruses built for gay club dance floors, where hi-NRG ruled the mid-80s. “Your Love” was the peak: four minutes of mechanical joy with a hook that treats the voice as one more synthesizer.

The hit and the second act

“Your Love” became a European club staple and a chart record in several markets, with the album Unexpected Lovers carrying the momentum; follow-up “Babe, We're Gonna Love Tonight” kept Lime on dance floors for years. In North America, Lime remained a DJ's record rather than a radio one — a fate shared by nearly all hi-NRG, which was segregated onto club playlists even as it invented sounds mainstream pop would soon absorb. The duo continued recording through the 80s and 90s and later re-emerged as part of the 2000s dance revival, when “Your Love” was rediscovered by producers raised on its sequencers.

The legacy

Lime's afterlife is double. First, the direct lineage: the punchy, bass-led dance production the LePages pioneered echoes through decades of club music, and “Your Love” still appears on 80s dance compilations and in DJ sets that want the real vintage voltage. Second, the historical correction: Montreal was an early North American hub of electronic dance music — a fact the city's later electronic renaissance makes easy to forget. Lime belongs in the same sentence as Trans-X in the export ledger: European-facing, machine-made, unmistakably Canadian in its under-the-radar persistence.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Lime?

A Montreal hi-NRG duo — Denis and Denyse LePage — who wrote, produced, and recorded their own dance records in the 1980s.

What was Lime’s biggest hit?

“Your Love” (1985), a European club and chart success, followed by “Babe, We're Gonna Love Tonight.”

What is hi-NRG?

A fast, synth-driven dance music style popular in gay clubs in the 80s; Canada, and Montreal in particular, produced a small wave of internationally respected hi-NRG records.
