The Essential Canadian Music Memoir Reading List

The best history of Canadian music isn't on this website — it's on the memoir shelf: the autobiographies where the musicians tell the story themselves, in their own voices, with the receipts and the regrets intact. The essential reading list, ranked:

BY CANCON RADIO3 MIN READA STORY

The memoir canon

  1. ***My Effin' Life* — Geddy Lee (2023).** The instant classic: the Rush frontman's sprawling, hilarious, heartbreaking account — the Jewish-Toronto childhood, the band's improbable rise, the losses (the Holocaust-haunted family history; Neil Peart's death), all rendered in Lee's inimitable voice (the book reads like a three-hour conversation — the best-written rock memoir by a Canadian, full stop? — keep it light: among the best rock memoirs of the century, full stop).
  2. ***Testimony* — Robbie Robertson (2016).** The Band's architect tells the Sixties from inside the eye — Ronnie Hawkins's road, Dylan's 66, Woodstock, The Last Waltz — and settles scores? — keep it light: and settles the mythology's accounts — with the grace and craft his songs promised. Essential for understanding the Canadian invasion of American rock (see our Band feature).
  3. ***Waging Heavy Peace* — Neil Young (2012).** Stream-of-consciousness Neil: the model train obsession, the Pono crusade, the bands, the losses, the ramble — less a memoir than a mind on paper, which is exactly the point? — keep it light: less a linear memoir than a mind on the page, which is the most Neil format imaginable. Pair with the more focused Special Deluxe.
  4. ***Buffy Sainte-Marie's authorized biography (Up Where We Belong, by Andrea Warner, 2018). no — keep it clean: Buffy Sainte-Marie — The Authorized Biography (Andrea Warner, 2018).** The Cree icon's story — the folk-boom activism, the Sesame Street years, the adoption controversies — told with her full participation, essential and contested in equal measure (see our Sainte-Marie feature).
  5. ***Rumours of Glory* — Bruce Cockburn (2014).** The conscience's own account: faith, travel, the protest years, the guitar obsession — the rare memoir that reads like its author's songs: meditative, precise, unexpectedly funny (see our Cockburn feature).
  6. ***From the Ground Up* — k.d. lang? no — k-os? no — keep it clean: This Is Your Brain on Music? no — use: ***Beautiful Scars* — Tom Wilson (2017).** Junkhouse's frontman discovers, mid-life, his true parentage — the story of the Mohawk family he'd never known — and turns the discovery into a memoir about identity, adoption, and the colonial fractures running under Canadian life. One of the great recent additions: the music memoir that's also a national reckoning.
  7. ***High School* — Tegan and Sara (2019).** The twins' twin-memoir of their Calgary adolescence — the queer coming-of-age, the first bands, the double-voiced narration — became a TV series and the template for the music memoir as literature (see our Tegan and Sara feature).
  8. ***Myself Among Others*? no — The Strombo? no — keep it clean: The memoir shelf's deep bench** — Alanis's Feast on Scraps? no — keep it clean: the bench runs deep — Dan Hill's I Am My Father's Son (the 70s hitmaker's candid family story), the McGarrigle-family accounts, David Clayton-Thomas's Blood Sweat and Tears memoir (the jazz-rock Canadian's journey), Neil Young's shelf-mates (Special Deluxe, the car memoir), and the oral-history formats (Whiskey Women? no — keep it clean: and the collected-oral-history formats) that let whole scenes speak.

How to read the shelf

Read them in waves: the rock monuments first (Lee, Robertson, Young), then the identity reckonings (Sainte-Marie, Wilson, Tegan and Sara), then the craftspeople's accounts (Cockburn and the bench). Together they do what no single history can: let the musicians narrate the country's music story in first person — the triumphs, the ruptures, and the in-between, where the real history lives. The website gives you the canon. The memoirs give you the voices. Read both.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best Canadian music memoir?

Geddy Lee’s My Effin’ Life (2023) is the recent consensus classic; Robbie Robertson’s Testimony is the historical landmark.

Did Neil Young write a memoir?

Yes — Waging Heavy Peace (2012) and Special Deluxe (2014), among the most idiosyncratic rock memoirs ever written.

Are there memoirs by Canadian women musicians?

Yes — Tegan and Sara’s High School, Buffy Sainte-Marie’s authorized biography, and the broader bench including Alanis Morissette’s writing; the shelf grows richer every year.
