“Red”: Treble Charger's Sharpest Turn

Treble Charger had already lived one full life before “Red.” The band began in the early 90s as a noisy, fuzz-drenched indie outfit — darlings of campus radio with albums like nc17 and Self Title — and then, mid-decade, turned hard toward melody and polish. “Red” announced the new incarnation: a gleaming, driving single that made the band staples of Canadian rock radio and video rotation.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

The turn

“Red” rides one of the great opening guitar riffs of 90s Canadian rock — chunky, descending, instantly memorable — into a chorus calibrated for both rock radio and MuchMusic. The lyric is romantic frustration rendered in primary colors: direct, a little desperate, perfectly pitched for the era's alternative-to-mainstream pipeline. The parent album Maybe It's Me (1997) went gold in Canada, and Treble Charger suddenly occupied the slot the market was building for a Canadian band: credible enough for the indie alumni, accessible enough for the crossover audience that would soon be called pop-punk.

Before and after

The earlier Treble Charger — scrappy, feedback-friendly — still has its loyalists, and the pivot had its costs (original fans heard a sellout; the band heard a future). The follow-up Detox (2002) delivered “Hundred Million,” their biggest Canadian hit, and for a few years Treble Charger were omnipresent: radio, videos, tours, the full machine. Then, as with so many mid-tier rock acts of the era, the industry shifted — labels consolidated, formats changed — and the band wound down quietly in the mid-2000s, reuniting years later for anniversary shows that proved the catalog's durability.

The legacy

“Red” remains the pivot point: the moment a noise band became a singles band, and the song that still pulls a crowd. It's a case study in the 90s Canadian pipeline — campus radio to MuchMusic to gold records — and proof that the turn, however debated, produced songs people kept. The riff outlived the argument.

Frequently asked questions

Who sings “Red”?

Treble Charger, the band led by Greig Nori and Bill Priddle, from their 1997 album Maybe It's Me.

What was Treble Charger’s biggest hit?

“Hundred Million” (2002) was their biggest chart single; “Red” is the signature 90s record.

What happened to Treble Charger?

After the mid-2000s wind-down, the band has reunited periodically for shows celebrating their catalog.
