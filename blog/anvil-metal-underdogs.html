Anvil!: The Story Behind Metal's Most Beloved Underdogs

Anvil — the Toronto metal band formed in 1978 by Steve "Lips" Kudlow and Robb Reiner — should have been famous. For thirty years, they weren't. Then a 2008 documentary made them the most beloved underdogs in metal history. The story of the band that never quit:

BY CANCON RADIO3 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the pioneers (1978–1983)

Anvil formed in Toronto in 1978 — part of the first wave of heavy metal, before the genre had a name. The early records — Hard 'n' Heavy (1981), Metal on Metal (1982), Forged in Fire (1983) — established the sound: speed-metal precision, Lips's guitar theatrics (playing with a vibrator, the famous stage antics), and the work ethic that would define them. They toured with Metallica, Slayer, and Bon Jovi; they influenced the thrash generation (Metallica, Anthrax, and Slayer have all cited them); they were, by any measure, pioneers.

Chapter two: the wilderness (1984–2008)

And then — nothing. The mainstream passed them by. The 80s became the 90s; the grunge era erased the metal they'd helped invent. Anvil kept playing: small clubs, European festivals (where they retained a cult following), day jobs (Lips delivered food for a Toronto caterer; Reiner worked construction). The records kept coming — self-financed, self-released, the work ethic unbroken. The band became the definition of the working metal band: the one that never stopped, regardless of whether anyone was watching.

Chapter three: the documentary and the redemption (2008)

In 2005, a former roadie-turned-filmmaker named Sacha Gervasi — who'd worked for Anvil as a teenager — began filming the band. The result, Anvil! The Story of Anvil (2008), was a documentary masterpiece: the story of two middle-aged men who'd never given up on the dream, told with humor, heart, and not a hint of condescension. The film became a Sundance sensation, a box-office success, and a cultural phenomenon — the underdog story that resonated far beyond metal. Anvil became famous — thirty years late, but famous: the arenas they'd dreamed of, the audiences they'd deserved, the recognition that had been owed since 1982.

Chapter four: the beloved institution (2008–present)

The years since the documentary have found Anvil exactly where they always wanted to be: touring the world, recording new records (Juggernaut of Justice, Hope in Hell, Pounding the Pavement, Legal at Last, Impact Is Imminent), and occupying the unique position of metal's most beloved underdogs — the band that never quit, finally getting the credit. The documentary didn't change the band; it changed the world's view of the band. The work ethic that got them through the wilderness is the same one that sustains them now. The vibrator guitar is still in the act. The metal still pounds.

Why they matter

They matter because the Anvil story is the purest version of the working-musician ethos: the band that never gave up, never compromised, never stopped — and was finally, belatedly, recognized. The documentary didn't just make them famous; it made them symbols — of persistence, of passion, of the dream deferred but never abandoned. The pioneers got their due. The underdogs got their day. The metal never stopped.

Frequently asked questions

Who are Anvil?

A Toronto metal band (formed 1978) — pioneers of speed metal, subject of the 2008 documentary Anvil! The Story of Anvil.

What is Anvil’s best album?

Metal on Metal (1982) is the classic; the documentary-era records (Juggernaut of Justice, Hope in Hell) document the comeback.

Is Anvil still together?

Yes — Lips and Reiner continue to record and tour, the work ethic undimmed.
