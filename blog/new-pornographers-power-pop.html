The New Pornographers: Power Pop as a Group Project

A supergroup that's actually a band; a band whose membership keeps shifting and whose sound never does; a catalog of power-pop so consistently excellent that critics ran out of superlatives three albums in. The New Pornographers — Vancouver's great ensemble, built by A.C. Newman with Neko Case and Dan Bejar (among others) — are the Canadian indie institution that treats pop craft as a collective discipline. The results: eight albums, two decades, zero duds.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: Mass Romantic (2000)

The supergroup thesis, proven instantly: Newman (the Zumpano songwriter with the melody gift), Case (the volcano? — keep it clean: the powerhouse voice, then rising as a solo artist), Bejar (Destroyer's arch art-pop auteur), and a rhythm section of Vancouver veterans — assembled for a one-off? — no: assembled with intent, and delivering Mass Romantic (2000): "Letter from an Occupant," "Mass Romantic," "The Slow Descent into Alcoholism" — power-pop engineered to ecstatic precision, melodies stacked like architecture, and a band chemistry that made the "supergroup" label feel inadequate (see our supergroups feature). Critics reached for decade-best language on arrival. The band had to be a going concern. It became one.

Chapter two: the imperial catalog (2003–2010)

The run that followed established the band as the most reliable album-artists of the Canadian indie wave: Electric Version (2003) — tighter, brighter, "The Laws Have Changed"; Twin Cinema (2005) — many fans' pick for the summit, "Use It," "Sing Me Spanish Techno"; Challengers (2007) — deeper, duskier; Together (2010) — the communal statement. Through it all, the group's sound held constant — stacked harmonies, Newman's chiming guitar figures, Bejar's sideways interjections, Case's choruses — while the members' solo careers (Case's alt-country ascent, Bejar's Destroyer odysseys, Newman's own records) orbited without ever eclipsing the mothership. The "band as group project" model worked because the members kept other outlets: the Pornographers remained the place where the melodies went to become communal.

Chapter three: the perpetual reunion

The 2010s and 2020s — Brill Bruisers (2014), Whiteout Conditions (2017), In the Morse Code of Brake Lights (2019), Continue as a Guest (2023) — extended the no-duds streak into a third decade, the lineup flux (Bejar's periodic absences, Case's selective touring, the rotating cast) absorbed into the band's identity: the New Pornographers are less a fixed group than a permanent possibility, convened whenever Newman's melodies demand it. The result is the healthiest supergroup in Canadian music — one that never broke up, never disappointed, and never stopped treating power pop as high art. "Sing me Spanish techno" indeed — whatever it means, it sings.

Why they matter

They matter because they proved the supergroup could be sustainable — that collective craft, flexible membership, and unrelenting melodic standards could produce two decades of excellence without a single collapse. In the Canadian indie canon, they're the band other bands' songs want to sound like: the melody masters, the harmony architects, the permanent possibility. Power pop as a group project — the group's still meeting. The melodies keep winning.

Frequently asked questions

Who are the New Pornographers?

A Vancouver power-pop band/supergroup centered on A.C. Newman, with Neko Case, Dan Bejar, and a rotating cast of West Coast musicians.

What is the New Pornographers’ best album?

Mass Romantic (2000) is the landmark debut; Twin Cinema (2005) is the fan-favorite summit; the no-duds catalog runs eight albums deep.

Are the New Pornographers still together?

Yes — the band continues recording (most recently Continue as a Guest, 2023) with its flexible-membership model intact.
