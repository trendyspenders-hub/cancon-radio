Storm-Cancelled: Legendary Shows the Weather Won

In most countries, concert cancellations are caused by bands. In Canada, they're caused by meteorology — the blizzard that swallowed the highway, the lightning that cleared the festival field, the prairie wind that turned the stage into a sail? — keep it light: the prairie wind that made the stage untenable. The shows the weather won are a national genre unto themselves: tragic, comic, and deeply Canadian. The legend of the storms:

The weather-cancellation canon

  1. The blizzard highway show — The classic: the band's bus stopped three hours out? — no: the classic: the storm closing the highway between the band and the city — the fans arriving at the venue to find the doors locked and the marquee already changed? — keep it light: the fans arriving to find the doors locked, the band snowbound in another province, the night becoming a bar story for five hundred people at once (the audience that bonded over the cancellation — the storm's accidental gift — see our meltdowns feature).
  2. The lightning-clear — The outdoor show evacuated mid-set (the festival's safety protocol doing its job — the crowd shuffling to the parking-lot cars in the rain, the artist promising to return — keep it light: the crowd shuffling to their cars, the artist vowing to return): the festival edition of the genre, weather interrupting at festival scale (the headliner cut mid-chorus — see our festival features).
  3. The -40° decision — The prairie and northern shows cancelled not by the storm itself but by the ambient cold (the equipment failing, the fingers refusing — keep it light: the equipment failing in the deep cold, the outdoor stage untenable at forty below): the Canadian version of the rain check — "we'll reschedule for literally any other season."
  4. The ice-storm blackouts — The great ice storms (the 1998 Eastern Canada ice storm being the epochal example — keep it light: the great ice storms of Eastern Canadian memory) shutting down whole regions' power — the shows cancelled not by weather-at-the-venue but by weather-at-the-grid: the entire circuit dark for a week, the rescheduled shows cascading into spring (see our storm stories).
  5. The near-misses that became legends — The shows that almost cancelled and played anyway (the band arriving through the storm, the heroic load-in, the under-attended? — keep it light: the heroic load-in, the storm-surviving audience becoming a cult for life): the flip side — the shows where weather heightened the night (see our venue features for the room-bonding effect).

Why weather cancellations are our genre

Weather cancellations are the Canadian live-music genre because they're communal by design: the shared disappointment (the storm took something from everyone at once — see our folk features) makes strangers into a crowd even in defeat, and the rescheduled show — when it comes — arrives as a victory (the storm having tested the commitment, the audience arriving battle-tested and loud — keep it light: the audience arriving louder for having survived the wait). Every Canadian music fan carries a storm story: the night the band didn't play, the highway that closed, the marquee in the snow. The weather wins sometimes. The music comes back always. Check the forecast. Bring a toque. See you at the reschedule.

Frequently asked questions

Have Canadian concerts been cancelled by weather?

Constantly — blizzards, lightning, deep cold, and ice storms have cancelled shows and festivals across the country (the genre is distinctly Canadian).

What happens when a Canadian festival is storm-cancelled?

Safety evacuations, rescheduling cascades, and — famously — the crowd-bonding of the shared disappointment (see our festival features).

What’s the most famous weather cancellation in Canadian music?

The 1998 ice storm darkened the entire Eastern Canadian circuit — the epochal example of the genre (see our meltdowns feature).
