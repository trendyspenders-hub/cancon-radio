Dine Alone Records: The Hamilton Upstart That Went National

Every Canadian indie label story is some version of "started in a basement." Dine Alone's twist is that the basement was in Hamilton, the founder was a teenager running concert promotions out of his bedroom, and the label he built would become the standard-bearer for modern Canadian rock — City and Colour, Alexisonfire, the Sheepdogs, and a roster that reads like a festival poster. The steel city's work ethic, in label form.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the bedroom promoter (2003–2005)

Joel Carriere began as what Hamilton produces naturally: a hustler with no Plan B. As a teenager he promoted all-ages shows around the Hammer? — keep it clean: around Hamilton, learning the live business from the ground up: booking, advancing, settling, doing it again next weekend. In 2005 he turned that muscle into a label, naming it Dine Alone — after a lyric by his favorite band, the Silver Jews? — keep it clean: after a lyric he loved — and the early releases came straight from the shows he was already promoting: Hamilton and Southern Ontario post-hardcore and punk.

Chapter two: the national roster (2005–2015)

Dine Alone's growth tracked the 2000s Canadian rock wave with remarkable precision. Alexisonfire — the St. Catharines post-hardcore five-piece whose dual-vocal chaos filled arenas — became the label's calling card. City and Colour (Dallas Green's solo turn from Alexisonfire) gave Dine Alone its singer-songwriter pillar: Sometimes, Bring Me Your Love, The Hurry and the Harm — records that turned Green into one of the most beloved voices in Canadian music. Then the rock-and-roll lane widened: The Sheepdogs (the Saskatoon band that famously gate-crashed the US charts via a Rolling Stone cover contest), Monster Truck (Hamilton's own riff machine), The Dirty Nil (the award-winning? — keep it clean: the ferocious Dundas? no — the ferocious punk power trio), and Canadian releases for international acts. By the 2010s, Dine Alone wasn't a regional upstart. It was a national institution with distribution muscle and a festival's worth of headliners.

Chapter three: the Hamilton way

What Dine Alone kept from its bedroom years is the Hamilton signature: artist-first, live-first, no-BS. Carriere's background as a promoter meant the label always understood that records are half the business — the other half is rooms, and Dine Alone acts tour like it. The label also stayed proudly Hamilton-based as the city's scene boomed, a symbiosis with the Arkells era that made the city and the label each other's proof of concept.

The legacy

Dine Alone demonstrated that the 2000s indie playbook — build from live shows, sign what your city loves, grow nationally without selling — had a second act in the streaming age, built on rock bands rather than blog buzz. It's the connective tissue between the MuchMusic generation and the playlist generation: Canadian rock, independently held, loud as ever.

Frequently asked questions

Who founded Dine Alone Records?

Joel Carriere, who began as a teenage concert promoter in Hamilton and launched the label in 2005.

What artists are on Dine Alone?

Alexisonfire, City and Colour, The Sheepdogs, Monster Truck, and The Dirty Nil anchor the roster, plus international acts' Canadian releases.

Is Dine Alone still independent?

Yes — it remains independently owned and Hamilton-based, still signing and developing Canadian rock.
