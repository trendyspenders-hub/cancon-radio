Barrymore's: Ottawa's Surviving Theatre

In a city that keeps its government buildings forever and its music venues for about fifteen minutes, Barrymore's is the glorious exception: a century-old theatre in the ByWard Market that became — and has remained — Ottawa's most storied live-music stage. The capital's scene has flowed around it for generations, and the old room, with its balcony and its ghosts? — keep it clean: with its balcony and its ornate bones, keeps the receipts.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the palace

Barrymore's began life in the 1920s as a movie palace — one of the grand picture-houses of the era, built with balconies, ornament, and the full theatrical dignity of silent-film's golden age. The movies eventually moved on, but the room remained: too beautiful to demolish, too well-proportioned to ignore. When live music claimed it in the decades that followed, the theatre found its second calling — and its calling card: a proper stage in a real theatre, in a capital that has never had enough of either.

Chapter two: the scene's home

Through the capital's successive music eras — the early-rock years, the punk and new wave waves, the 90s alternative boom, the modern indie circuit — Barrymore's functioned as Ottawa's unofficial headquarters: the room where touring acts played when they played Ottawa, and where local bands measured themselves against a real stage. Its size (hundreds, not thousands), its theatre bones, and its Market location made it the city's musical crossroads — the venue where the punk kids, the blues faithful, and the suburban 90s alterna-teens? — keep it clean: where the punk kids, the blues faithful, and the rock crowds all shared the same balcony sightlines.

Chapter three: survival as statement

That Barrymore's still operates — still booking, still standing, still beautiful — is, in Ottawa, a statement. The capital has lost venue after venue to development and drift (see our Ottawa-scene feature); the old theatre in the Market endures as the link between the capital's musical past and its present, hosting new generations of bands in the same room where their scene's grandparents? — keep it clean: in the same room where their city's earlier scenes made their noise. Some venues have histories. Barrymore's has strata.

Why it matters

Cities need rooms with memory, and Barrymore's is Ottawa's: the surviving theatre where the capital's music keeps its continuity, one show at a time. The government changes every few years. The room, mercifully, does not.

Frequently asked questions

What is Barrymore’s?

A historic 1920s theatre in Ottawa’s ByWard Market, operating for decades as the capital’s storied live-music venue.

How old is Barrymore’s?

The building dates to the 1920s as a movie palace; it has operated as a music venue for decades since.

Is Barrymore’s still open?

Yes — still operating as one of Ottawa’s key live-music rooms.
