Stars: Set Yourself on Fire and Montreal's Wounded Pop

Every scene needs a band that treats heartbreak as a craft material — not a confessional, but a discipline: polished, layered, and devastating at controlled temperature. For Montreal's indie wave, that band is Stars: the two-singer ensemble (Torquil Campbell and Amy Millan) who spent two decades perfecting a sound that could only be described as wounded pop — sad songs that shimmer, breakup albums you dance to, melancholy at its most meticulously arranged.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the broken social scene

Stars formed in Toronto but became themselves in Montreal — and within the Broken Social Scene orbit (Millan and Campbell are core members of the collective — see our BSS feature), they carved out the specific lane: where BSS was communal maximalism, Stars was intimate maximalism — boy-girl duets, electronic textures, strings, and the drama of two voices negotiating desire's negotiations. The early records (Nightsongs, Heart) built the cult; the sound was already unmistakable: every heartbreak rendered in hi-fi.

Chapter two: Set Yourself on Fire (2004)

The breakthrough — Set Yourself on Fire (2004) — was the canonical statement: "Ageless Beauty," "Your Ex-Lover Is Dead" (the title alone a mission statement — and the orchestral drama to match), "One More Night," "Calendar Girl." The album's signature: cold surfaces, warm blood — electronic precision wrapped around lyrics of genuine damage, delivered by two singers who sound like they're breaking up in real time and harmonizing about it. It became the era's definitive breakup record (see our breakup-song feature) and established Stars as Montreal's premier exponents of beautiful sadness.

Chapter three: the long consummation

The catalog since has been remarkably consistent: In Our Bedroom After the War (2007), The North (2011), No One Is Lost (2014), There Is No Love in Fluorescent Light (2017) — each a new variation on the same essential wager: that pop can hold sadness at album length and survive. The band's live shows — theatrical, luminous, communal — complete the picture: wounded pop as shared experience, the audience singing the sad parts together until they turn warm. Twenty years in, Stars remain the proof that melancholy, practiced faithfully, becomes a kind of joy.

Why they matter

Stars matter because they perfected a distinctly Canadian register — the cold-weather sad-pop that runs Mitchell→Cohen→the Weakerthans→Stars — and gave it a modern, electronic, two-voice form. Montreal's indie wave had many bands; it had only one that made heartbreak sound like home. Set yourself on fire — the instruction and the invitation.

Frequently asked questions

What is Stars’ best album?

Set Yourself on Fire (2004) is the consensus classic — the era’s defining breakup record.

Who sings in Stars?

Torquil Campbell and Amy Millan — the boy-girl duo at the band’s center (both also Broken Social Scene members).

Is Stars connected to Broken Social Scene?

Yes — Millan and Campbell are core members of the BSS collective (see our feature), with Stars as their own dedicated band.
