Carly Rae Jepsen: How "Call Me Maybe" Made Her Famous — and How Emotion Made Her Immortal

In 2012, Carly Rae Jepsen had the biggest song on earth, and the entire music industry agreed on what she was: a one-hit novelty act with a shelf life of about eighteen months. A decade later, "Call Me Maybe" is the least interesting thing about her. The real Carly Rae Jepsen story is the decade since: a songwriter who converted global fame into creative freedom, then quietly built one of the most devoted fanbases in pop.

Carly Rae Jepsen by the numbers - Born: November 21, 1985, Mission, British Columbia — Fraser Valley roots, musical theatre kid. - The launch: Third place on Canadian Idol in 2007; debut album Tug of War (2008) in Canada. - The phenomenon: "Call Me Maybe" — co-written with Josh Ramsay of Marianas Trench and Tavish Crowe — released 2011, global #1 in 2012, the best-selling single of that year worldwide, supercharged by the famous lip-sync video from Justin Bieber, Selena Gomez, and friends. - The cult: Emotion (2015) — critically rapturous, commercially modest, endlessly influential. - The run since: Dedicated (2019), The Loneliest Time (2022), The Loveliest Time (2023), Broadway's Cinderella (2014), and a touring career most pop stars would trade chart positions for.

Chapter one: Mission to mainstream

Jepsen grew up in the Fraser Valley doing musical theatre — a formative training ground she'd return to — and landed on the third season of Canadian Idol, finishing third. Her Canadian debut, Tug of War, was pleasant folk-pop; nothing about it predicted the tsunami. Then came "Call Me Maybe," a co-write with Marianas Trench's Josh Ramsay: a perfectly constructed crush song with strings, a spoken bridge, and a chorus engineered for involuntary singing. Bieber's cosign turned it into an event; the homemade lip-sync video turned it into a movement. It spent weeks at #1 across the planet and ended 2012 as the world's best-selling single.

Chapter two: the trap and the escape

Here's the part the industry didn't script: instead of chasing "Call Me Maybe 2," Jepsen pivoted to Broadway, starred in Cinderella, and then — crucially — spent years making the album she wanted. Kiss (2012) satisfied the label; Emotion (2015) satisfied the artist. Working with writers and producers from Dev Hynes to Sia to Rostam, she made a gleaming, wounded, 80s-inflected synth-pop record that critics received as a masterpiece and radio largely ignored. "Run Away with Me" — the saxophone-drenched opener — became the decade's most-beloved pop song that wasn't a hit.

Chapter three: the cult years

Emotion's commercial underperformance should have ended the story. Instead it began the second act. The album found its audience slowly — through word of mouth, through critical canonization, through the Emotion Side B EP of outtakes that fans treat as a proper album. Jepsen leaned in: more theatre, more careful albums (Dedicated, The Loneliest Time), and a live show honed into one of pop's most joyful. Her fanbase — disproportionately critics, musicians, and queer pop fans — isn't large the way arena pop is large; it's deep, the way cults are deep. Pop stars chase scale. Jepsen built loyalty, which turns out to be rarer.

Why Emotion became a classic

The case rests on craft: every song is written from inside a feeling rather than about it, and the production threads 80s references through modern pop without nostalgia cosplay. "Run Away with Me," "Emotion," "Your Type," "Boy Problems" — these are songs other pop writers study. A decade on, Emotion regularly tops "best pop albums of the 2010s" reader polls, and its influence surfaces wherever pop chooses sincerity over cynicism.

Frequently asked questions

What was Carly Rae Jepsen's big hit?

"Call Me Maybe" (2011/2012) — a worldwide #1 and the best-selling single of 2012 globally.

Is Emotion a good album?

Critically, one of the most acclaimed pop albums of the 2010s; commercially modest at release, it's since been canonized as a cult classic.

What has Carly Rae Jepsen done since "Call Me Maybe"?

Broadway's Cinderella, albums including Emotion, Dedicated, and The Loneliest Time, and a reputation as one of pop's best live performers.
