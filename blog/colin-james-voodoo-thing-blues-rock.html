Colin James: "Voodoo Thing" and the Three-Decade Crossover

Colin James is the answer to a trick question: which Canadian artist scored a top-40 American rock hit as a teenager, kept charting at home for three decades, jumped from blues to rock to jump-blues swing and back, and became one of the most quietly decorated live acts in the country? Regina's favorite son — a genuine blues prodigy who got his start sitting in with the masters before he could vote — built a career on the one thing that outlasts every trend: being an absolutely killer player.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the prodigy (1980s)

James Munn? — no: born Colin James Munn in Regina, 1964 — fell for blues early and pursued it with a prairie ferocity: learning at the feet of the touring American bluesmen who passed through Saskatchewan, earning sit-ins as a teenager, and landing in Vancouver's scene as a young gun with an old soul. The self-titled debut (1988) detonated: "Voodoo Thing" — produced with rock-radio muscle — became a genuine hit on both sides of the border, and the kid from Regina was suddenly the most exciting young blues-rock guitarist in North America. "Why'd You Lie" kept the momentum; the sophomore Sudden Stop (1990) delivered "Just Came Back," one of the great Canadian rock singles of its era.

Chapter two: the chameleon decades

What followed was a career built on refusal to be filed. James swung hard into jump-blues with the Little Big Band (mid-90s), winning new audiences and awards with a brass-powered jump sound most artists couldn't touch; he returned to hard blues, then rock, then acoustic roots, then back again — each phase anchored by the guitar playing, which critics consistently ranked among the best in the business. Through it all, the Canadian charts kept answering: multiple platinum and gold records, Juno awards across categories, and a live reputation that made him a festival fixture from coast to coast.

Chapter three: the elder statesman

Three decades in, James occupies the role every genre hopes for: the credible elder. He still records (blues records of the first order), still tours with crack bands, and still plays like the prodigy he was — the technique undimmed, the taste deepening. Younger Canadian blues players cite him as the bridge between the tradition and the mainstream, the proof that you can honor the masters and still get played on rock radio.

Why he matters

Colin James matters because longevity this graceful is rare: a career that began as teenage prodigy hype matured into one of the most respected catalogs in Canadian music, spanning blues, rock, and swing without ever sounding like tourism. "Voodoo Thing" opened the door; three decades of playing kept the house full.

Frequently asked questions

What was Colin James’s biggest hit?

“Voodoo Thing” (1988) was the breakout; “Just Came Back” (1990) is the signature Canadian rock single.

Where is Colin James from?

Regina, Saskatchewan — a prairie blues prodigy who sat in with American blues masters as a teenager.

What is the Little Big Band?

Colin James’s jump-blues project of the mid-90s — a brass-powered swing outfit that won him a second wave of fans and awards.
