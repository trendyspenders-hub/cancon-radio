“Hayloft”: Mother Mother’s Accidental Second Life

Few songs have lived two full lives as different as “Hayloft.” Mother Mother recorded it in 2008 for their album O My Heart — a twitchy, harmony-drenched indie-rock track with a sinister streak — and it spent a dozen years as a deep cut. Then, in the pandemic years, the internet found it, and “Hayloft” became one of the strangest resurrection stories in Canadian music.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Act one: the original

Mother Mother — the Vancouver band built around Ryan Guldemond's songwriting and the stacked harmonies of Molly Guldemond and Jasmin Parkin — made O My Heart (2008) with producer Howard Redekopp, and “Hayloft” sat near its center: a minor-key stomper with folk-song bones and art-pop angles, equal parts barn dance and bad dream. It was beloved by fans and critics; commercially, it was an album track on a well-regarded indie record. The band did what working Canadian bands do: toured, recorded more albums (Eureka, The Sticks, Very Good Bad Thing), built a devoted following, and became one of the country's most reliable live acts.

Act two: the algorithm

Around 2020, “Hayloft” began circulating on TikTok — its eerie hook and yelping harmonies perfect raw material for the platform's dark-cabaret aesthetic. Streams multiplied into the hundreds of millions; the song charted internationally years after release; a new, younger audience discovered the band wholesale, often without knowing they were Canadian, or that the song predated the trend by over a decade. Mother Mother leaned in gracefully — a “Hayloft II” companion single for the fans, tours reoriented to welcome the deluge of newcomers — and the band found itself, improbably, bigger in its second decade than its first.

Why it stuck

“Hayloft” rewards both audiences. For 2008 ears, it's artful indie rock with unusual harmonies and a sinister narrative edge. For 2020 ears, it's the perfect piece of strange — a song that sounds like a ritual and a pop chorus at once. The lesson isn't that TikTok revives old songs; it's that great Canadian songs, given a second chance, rarely waste it. The hayloft was always full. It just took the internet twelve years to open the door.

Frequently asked questions

Who sings “Hayloft”?

Mother Mother, the Vancouver indie-rock band fronted by Ryan Guldemond with Molly Guldemond and Jasmin Parkin on harmonies.

When did “Hayloft” become popular?

Originally released in 2008 on O My Heart, it went viral on TikTok around 2020 and became an international streaming hit more than a decade later.

What is Mother Mother’s biggest song?

“Hayloft,” by a wide margin in the streaming era; earlier singles like “Bit by Bit” and “Get Out the Way” were Canadian radio staples.
