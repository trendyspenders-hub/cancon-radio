Canada's Greatest Commercial Jingles, Remembered

The earworm is Canadian too: the ad music that colonized a nation's memory. The greatest, remembered:

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READA STORY

The jingles

  1. "I Am Canadian" — Molson (2000) — technically a rant, not a jingle, but the citizen-creed that outlived the brand. Two minutes of advertising that became the nation's second anthem.
  2. The Hinterland Who's Who theme — the classical flute behind every woodland-creature documentary, teaching a generation the word "loon."
  3. The CBC ident music — the public broadcaster's sonic signature: trust in three notes.
  4. The Hockey Night in Canada theme (Dolores Claman, 1968) — technically a broadcast theme but functionally the national jingle — the country's most recognized melody.
  5. "Why buy a mattress anywhere else?" — Sleep Country Canada — the slogan-as-jingle that became a national catchphrase, proof that a question can be an earworm.

Why ad music matters

The jingle (see our TV-themes feature) is the most concentrated audio-branding form ever invented: seconds to own a memory forever. Canada's jingles did it better than most — because the best of them weren't selling products, they were selling belonging. "I Am Canadian" sold beer by selling the country. That's the trick — and the songs knew it.

Frequently asked questions

What is Canada’s most famous jingle?

The Molson "I Am Canadian" rant and the Hinterland Who's Who theme top any honest ranking.

Do jingles still work?

The sonic-branding tradition continues — shorter, weirder, but the memory-ownership goal is identical.

What makes a great jingle?

The same thing that makes a great song: specificity, sincerity, and a hook you can't shake.
