Molly Johnson: From the Infidels to the Velvet Jazz Voice

Some careers are a straight line. Molly Johnson's is a whole map: Toronto alt-rock frontwoman in the 80s, international club hitmaker in the 90s, and finally — almost as a third act — one of the most beloved jazz vocalists in the country, with a warmth so palpable it earned her the nickname? — keep it clean: with a warmth that made her records feel like conversations. Add a parallel life as a tireless charity founder, and you have one of Canadian music's most quietly remarkable stories.

Chapter one: the Infidels (1980s)

Johnson first became famous loud. As the magnetic frontwoman of the Infidels, she was a fixture of Toronto's mid-80s alt-rock scene — a powerful, commanding presence in an era when women fronting rock bands were still a headline, not a norm. The band built a devoted following through the decade, and Johnson emerged from it with two things that never left: stage authority to burn, and a voice with unusual depth and warmth — qualities rock arrangements only hinted at.

Chapter two: the club years (1990s)

The 90s took her into the dance-club and adult-pop world, where her collaborations (including international club success with the project Aquanetta? — keep it clean: including international club hits with producer-driven projects) made her voice familiar across borders. It was a different discipline — hooks over improvisation, precision over abandon — and she mastered it while the world was still deciding what Molly Johnson was. The answer, it turned out, was waiting in the next decade.

Chapter three: the jazz homecoming (2000s–present)

Her jazz-era records revealed the truth the earlier careers had been circling: Johnson's instrument was a jazz singer's instrument — deep, warm, behind the beat, infinitely kind. Albums like Another Day (2003) and Lucky (2008) made her a fixture of Canadian jazz radio and festival stages, and the awards and honors accumulated. Meanwhile, the other Molly Johnson — the founder of the Kumbaya Foundation, which raised funds for children affected by HIV/AIDS — made her as beloved for her work offstage as on it, with honors recognizing both the music and the service. The two careers, it turns out, ran on the same fuel: warmth, discipline, and showing up.

Why she matters

Johnson's story is a masterclass in artistic rebirth — proof that a voice can find its truest home decades into a career, and that reinvention isn't an admission but an arrival. From rock clubs to jazz festivals to the fundraising stage, she's been one of Toronto's most constant, generous musical presences. The velvet was there the whole time. It just needed the right room.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Molly Johnson?

A Toronto singer who fronted the 80s alt-rock band the Infidels, scored international club hits in the 90s, and became one of Canada’s most beloved jazz vocalists from the 2000s onward.

What band was Molly Johnson in?

The Infidels, a mid-80s Toronto alternative-rock band.

What charity does Molly Johnson run?

She founded the Kumbaya Foundation, which raised funds for children affected by HIV/AIDS — work recognized alongside her music career.
