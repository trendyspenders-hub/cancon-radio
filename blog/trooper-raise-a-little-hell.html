Trooper: Raise a Little Hell and the B.C. Party Circuit

Some bands have hits. Trooper has anthems — the kind of songs that don't just play at a B.C. party but are the party. For five decades, the Vancouver band's catalog has functioned as the unofficial soundtrack of the West Coast's good times: "We're Here for a Good Time (Not a Long Time)" on every dock and deck in the province, "Raise a Little Hell" as the universal permission slip. This is the story of Canada's most reliable good-time band.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the Main Street origins (1975)

Trooper formed in Vancouver in 1975 — singer Ra McGuire and guitarist Brian Smith writing songs with a rare gift: choruses that sounded like already-drunk singalongs on first listen. Their mid-70s run of albums built them as a Canadian AOR staple, and the live show — loose, grinning, built for beer gardens — made them the natural headliners of the West Coast circuit: the club-and-festival treadmill that, in B.C., has no off-season.

Chapter two: the anthem era (1978–1980)

The imperial run compressed into about three years. "We're Here for a Good Time (Not a Long Time)" (1978) arrived as a philosophy in three chords — carpe diem with a beer in hand — and instantly became the kind of song that outlives its chart run by decades. Then "Raise a Little Hell" (1978), "The Boys in the Bright White Sports Car," and "Two for the Show" completed the anthem stack: every one a working crowd-participation machine, every one still programmed on B.C. radio like a public utility. The albums went multi-platinum at home; the band toured endlessly; the catalog became provincial infrastructure.

Chapter three: the eternal Friday night

What happened next is the interesting part: nothing, and everything. Trooper never broke up dramatically, never burned out publicly — they just kept playing, the B.C. circuit's house band for half a century, the anthems compounding in cultural weight until the band became something rarer than a rock group: a tradition. Reunion-era lineups? — keep it clean: Continuing lineups and anniversary tours keep the Friday-night circuit stocked, and the songs keep doing their job — docks, decks, weddings, and one specific moment at every B.C. party when somebody yells the first line and everybody else supplies the rest.

Why they matter

Trooper matters because they perfected the functional anthem — music engineered not for the charts but for the crowd, songs whose whole job is to make strangers sing together. Canada's canon is full of artists who conquered the world; Trooper conquered something harder — every Friday night in British Columbia, forever. "A good time, not a long time"? The songs got it half right: the time was good, and it's been very long.

Frequently asked questions

What is Trooper’s biggest hit?

“We’re Here for a Good Time (Not a Long Time)” is the signature anthem; “Raise a Little Hell” and “The Boys in the Bright White Sports Car” complete the classic trio.

Where is Trooper from?

Vancouver, British Columbia — formed in 1975 by Ra McGuire and Brian Smith.

Is Trooper still together?

Yes — the band has continued touring for decades, a fixture of the B.C. live circuit and festival scene.
