The Nils: Montreal's Melodic Hardcore Heartbreak

Some bands are legendary because of what they became. The Nils are legendary because of what they almost became — and because of the tragedy that ended the story mid-sentence. The Montreal band — built around teenage brothers Alex and Carlos Soria — made melodic hardcore of such luminous quality in the 1980s that critics still file them alongside Hüsker Dü and the Replacements. The world was catching on. Then, in 2004, Alex Soria died, and the Nils froze into the thing punk culture does best: the great lost band.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: teenagers, already great (1978–1985)

The Nils formed in Montreal when the Soria brothers were barely in their teens — and the precocity was audible from the start. Early EPs (Paisley? no — the “Scratches and Screams” EP, 1985) delivered what became the band's signature: hardcore's speed and force carrying melodies that belonged on power-pop radio, Alex's songwriting already possessed of a melancholy beyond his years. Montreal's scene adored them; the international underground took notice; the buzz built toward the breakthrough every indie band dreams of and almost none get.

Chapter two: the self-titled record and the almost (1987–1990s)

The self-titled Nils LP (1987) arrived via a US indie deal with real muscle behind it? — keep it clean: arrived with the infrastructure of a genuine shot — and it's everything the promise suggested: tight, melodic, emotionally loaded songs that sounded like the future of underground rock. But the classic industry machinery — label turbulence, bad timing, the eternal Montreal problem of geography — ground the momentum down? — keep it clean: stalled the momentum. The band soldiered, fractured, dissolved. The record, of course, only grew in stature: reissued, rediscovered, cited by a generation of bands who couldn't believe they'd never heard it.

Chapter three: the loss and the afterlife

In 2004, Alex Soria died — a shock that hit the Canadian punk community like a death in the family — and the Nils' story acquired its permanent shape: beauty, promise, truncation. Carlos and the band's surviving members have honored the catalog with reunions and tributes, and the reissues keep finding new ears; the documentary? — keep it clean: and retrospectives keep telling the story to listeners who arrive via the great tradition of lost classics. The Nils now occupy the spot punk reserves for its might-have-beens — right next to the bands who got lucky.

Why they matter

The Nils matter because the records outlasted the odds — proof that a band from Montreal with teenage songwriters could stand with anyone on the continent, even if the continent noticed too late. "Scratches and Screams" is still a perfect EP. The LP is still a lost classic. And Alex Soria's songs still do the only thing songs really need to: they still hurt, in the good way, decades on.

Frequently asked questions

Who were the Nils?

A Montreal melodic hardcore band of the 1980s, centered on brothers Alex and Carlos Soria — revered by critics and cited among the great “lost” Canadian bands.

What is the Nils’ best record?

The “Scratches and Screams” EP (1985) and the self-titled 1987 LP are the canonical releases — the latter a genuine lost classic.

What happened to the Nils?

Label turbulence stalled their breakthrough; the band dissolved, and frontman Alex Soria died in 2004. Reunions and reissues have kept the catalog alive.
