“When Something Stands for Nothing”: The Headstones' Sneer, Perfected

The Headstones arrived in 1993 like a bar fight set to music: Kingston, Ontario's loudest export, fronted by Hugh Dillon — a singer who delivered every lyric like he'd been personally wronged by the subject. “When Something Stands for Nothing,” the title track of their debut Picture of Health, was the mission statement: sarcasm as anthem, doubt as a chant-along chorus.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

The song

Built on a bruising guitar riff and a rhythm section that hits like slamming doors, “When Something Stands for Nothing” turns postmodern cynicism into fist-pumping rock and roll — the trick the Headstones pulled better than anyone in 90s Canada. Dillon's vocal is a masterclass in controlled contempt: he sneers on the verses and belts the chorus like a confession he'd rather not make. The lyric skewers hollow symbols and empty authority, which is why it landed with a generation of Canadian rock fans raised on MuchMusic irony but hungry for something with actual teeth. The album went platinum in Canada, and the band became one of the country's most ferocious live acts.

The career

The Headstones' run through the 90s — Teeth and Tissue (1995), Smile and Wave (1996), Nickels for Your Nightmares (2000) — made them road warriors with a catalogue of radio records (“Cemetery,” “Absolutely,” “Three Angels,” and a bruising cover of the Traveling Wilburys' “Tweeter and the Monkey Man” that became a MuchMusic staple). When they wound down in the early 2000s, Dillon pivoted to acting with real success (he remains a familiar face on Canadian screens); the band's story seemed closed.

The second act

It wasn't. Reunion shows in the 2010s revealed an audience that had never left — bigger, in some markets, than the original run — and the Headstones resumed as a working band: new records (Love + Fury, 2013; Little Army, 2017; While Something Stands, 2023 — note the callback), festival slots, and Dillon still sneering with total commitment. “When Something Stands for Nothing” opens the story and, live, closes every argument. A sneer, it turns out, can be a lifelong instrument.

Frequently asked questions

Who are the Headstones?

A hard-rock band from Kingston, Ontario, fronted by Hugh Dillon — platinum-selling in Canada in the 90s and active again since their 2010s reunion.

What was the Headstones’ first album?

Picture of Health (1993), featuring the title-track anthem “When Something Stands for Nothing.”

What is Hugh Dillon doing now?

Both: he continues to front the reunited Headstones and maintains a parallel career as an actor in Canadian film and television.
