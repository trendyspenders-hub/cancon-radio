Chromeo: Montreal's Funk Revivalists

The great trick of Chromeo — the Montreal duo of David "Dave 1" Macklovitch and Patrick "P-Thugg" Gemayel — is that the irony and the sincerity are the same thing: they play 80s-style electro-funk with a wink, but the musicianship is dead serious, the hooks are real, and the love for the source material is total. Twenty years of "Fancy Footwork" later, they're one of the most beloved party institutions in Canadian music — the funk revivalists who made the whole thing work.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the Montreal brothers-in-funk

Macklovitch (a PhD-holding? — keep it light: the academic-turned-frontman — Dave 1 actually has a PhD in French literature — that's well documented; keep) and Gemayel met in Montreal in the early 2000s, bonding over a shared devotion to the 80s electro-funk canon (Zapp, Cameo, the Gap Band) — and built Chromeo as both homage and argument: the style deserved revival, and it deserved it played well. The early records (She's in Control, 2004; Fancy Footwork, 2007) established the formula: talk-box hooks, slap-bass synth lines, lyrics of hapless romantic pursuit — funk played with the skill of session pros and the grin of pop comedians.

Chapter two: the blog-era crossover

Chromeo were the fun wing of the Canadian electro wave (see our blog-house feature): "Fancy Footwork," "Needy Girl," "Night by Night" — songs that blog aggregators passed around and festival crowds adopted as party scripture. The duo's look (the matching glasses, the keytar-and-guitar staging) and persona (chatty, self-deprecating, genuinely funny) made them the era's most likable exports, and the musicianship kept them credible long after the blog wave receded: subsequent records (Business Casual, 2010; White Women, 2014; Head over Heels, 2018; Adult Contemporary, 2024) deepened the craft while keeping the grin. The live show — two frontmen sharing one microphone like the Brothers Johnson? — keep it clean: two frontmen trading talk-box and guitar across a mirror-image stage — became a festival institution.

Chapter three: the institution years

A decade-plus in, Chromeo occupy a rare perch: the band that outgrew its moment without abandoning its joke — the funk revival now simply the funk, Chromeo its most reliable North American custodians. Dave 1's parallel career (as a writer, academic, and taste-maker — the earnest half of the equation) and P-Thugg's production work (the duo write and produce their records entirely) complete the profile: the ironic-sincere duo who turned a genre exercise into a two-decade career. Fancy footwork, indeed — the feet kept dancing.

Why they matter

They matter because sincerity-through-irony is one of pop's hardest balances, and Chromeo have held it for twenty years — playing the 80s canon with the skill of believers and the joy of pranksters, and giving Canadian electronic music its funniest, funkiest corner. The wink is real. So is the funk. Both keep working.

Frequently asked questions

Who are Chromeo?

A Montreal electro-funk duo — Dave 1 (David Macklovitch) and P-Thugg (Patrick Gemayel) — known for 80s-style funk played with musicianship and wit.

What is Chromeo’s biggest song?

“Fancy Footwork” (2007) is the signature anthem; “Needy Girl” and “Night by Night” are the other classics.

Is Chromeo ironic or serious?

Both at once — the sincere-ironic balance is the whole act: the homage is played with believer-level skill and a comedian’s grin.
