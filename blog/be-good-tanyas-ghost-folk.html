The Be Good Tanyas: Vancouver's Ghost-Folk Trio

In the early 2000s, when "folk" was a dirty word in indie circles, a Vancouver trio made it haunting: the Be Good Tanyas — three voices and a handful of old-time instruments — built a sound so lonesome and lovely that it carried Canada's old-time revival around the world. "The Littlest Birds" became the generation's campfire anthem. The Tanyas became the revival's gentlest standard-bearers.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: Blue Horse (2000)

Samantha Parton and Trish Klein (with founding member Jolie Holland on the debut) assembled in Vancouver's folk underground (see our Vancouver features) and made Blue Horse (2000): eleven songs of old-time ballads, originals, and Carter Family ghosts, rendered in three-part harmony over guitar, banjo, and fiddle — recorded with a warmth that made it sound old and immediate simultaneously. "The Littlest Birds sing the prettiest songs" — the opening line and the signature — became the indie era's most beloved folk refrain: the rare song that's a philosophy, a comfort, and a singalong in three minutes. The album's run built a devoted international following (the UK folk scene embraced them early), and the trio's sound — ghostly, communal, pre-war in texture and modern in feel — defined the era's folk revival on both sides of the Atlantic.

Chapter two: the lonesome catalog

Chinatown (2003) and Hello Love (2006) deepened the aesthetic: the harmonies richer, the originals starker (Parton's songwriting becoming the catalog's anchor), the old-time sources more deeply digested. The live shows — three voices around one microphone? — keep it light: the trio's unamplified intimacy in small rooms — became legendary in folk circles: the kind of performances audiences describe in reverent tones, the kind that convert the skeptical to folk. The Tanyas never chased the mainstream (the classic Canadian way — see our Victoria feature); they simply became essential to everyone who cared.

Chapter three: the gentle afterlife

The band's later years were quieter — health pauses (Parton's recovery from a serious accident — keep it light: life interruptions), occasional reunions, and the catalog's steady glow — but the legacy settled into its final form: the revival's founding document (see our folk features), the harmony tradition's modern entry point, and proof that the oldest sounds, sung with love and tuned precisely, never date. The littlest birds are still singing. The songs are still the prettiest.

Why they matter

They matter because they made Canada's folk revival cool without trying — no irony, no costume, just three voices and the tradition — and opened the door for the trad wave that followed (see our features). In the great Canadian folk lineage (Creighton's collectors → the revival singers → the Tanyas → today), they're the hinge: the ones who carried the songbook into the indie era without dropping a single note.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Be Good Tanyas’ biggest song?

“The Littlest Birds” (2000) is the signature anthem — a campfire standard of the folk revival.

Who were the Be Good Tanyas?

A Vancouver folk trio — Samantha Parton and Trish Klein as the core — blending old-time music with original songwriting in signature three-part harmony.

Are the Be Good Tanyas still together?

The band has performed sporadically in later years (with life interruptions and occasional reunions), but the catalog’s legacy is permanent.
