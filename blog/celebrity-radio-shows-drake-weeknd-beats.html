Celebrity Radio Shows: How Two Torontonians Took Over Streaming Radio

When Apple launched its global streaming radio station in June 2015, the gamble was that a single digital channel — beamed to every iPhone on earth — could matter again. The station's first era didn't just validate the gamble; it made two Canadians its defining voices. Drake's OVO Sound Radio and The Weeknd's Memento Mori turned the celebrity radio show into a new kind of power: part mixtape, part press conference, part empire.

BY CANCON RADIO4 MIN READA TIMELINE

The timeline

June 30, 2015 — The launch, and Drake is the center of it

Apple's Beats 1 signs on with a global radio station built on personality programming — and Drake isn't just a guest; he's effectively the flagship artist, appearing on day one and immediately announcing OVO Sound Radio, his own recurring show. The first episode, weeks later, debuted “Hotline Bling” to the planet — the single premiering not on a streaming playlist or a leak but on his radio show, delivered by the artist himself. The move recaptured something the streaming era had stripped away: the appointment listen, the DJ as authority, the premiere as event.

2015–2017OVO Sound Radio becomes a launchpad

Across its run, OVO Sound Radio functioned as the empire's radio arm: new OVO signings premiered there, Drake debuted and re-debuted music at will (the show was a recurring weapon in his release strategy, surfacing tracks and remixes outside album cycles), and international guests — from grime MCs to UK rap's new wave — got a North American platform through a Toronto lens. The show mattered because it was curated: Drake playing what Drake loved, in a Toronto accent, with the city's scene as default rather than exotic. For the label, it was infrastructure; for fans, it was eavesdropping on the 6.

2018The Weeknd gets darker: Memento Mori

In June 2018, The Weeknd launched Memento Mori — a show with a Latin motto (remember you must die) that perfectly fit his brand of luxurious gloom. Where Drake's show was a label megaphone, Abel Tesfaye's was a mood: long atmospheric stretches, deep cuts, unreleased material surfacing like séances, and a window into the influences behind his sound — from new wave to Ethiopian music to the Toronto R&B underground that raised him. Episodes timed to his album cycles turned the show into the companion text of the After Hours era — the place where the persona spoke unmediated, between records, in his own sequencing.

2019–2021The format goes global

The celebrity radio show becomes standard infrastructure for streaming platforms: artists across genres launch residencies, and the playlist era absorbs the DJ era's lessons. But the Canadian footprint remains outsized — Toronto's ecosystem had understood the value of broadcast-style curation earlier than most, and OVO's model (label as radio network) gets studied and copied industry-wide. Meanwhile, both shows keep operating on their own irregular rhythms — drops rather than schedules, events rather than programming — which turns out to be the streaming-native version of “appointment radio.”

2022–presentThe legacy phase

As Beats 1 evolves and competitors consolidate, the celebrity radio show settles into its permanent role: less a weekly habit than a weapon — deployed for premieres, rollouts, and world-building. Drake and The Weeknd's shows remain the format's canonical examples, for a simple reason: they were first, they were biggest, and they were both, unmistakably, Canadian voices programming the world's airtime.

Why the celebrity radio show mattered

  1. It restored the premiere. Before these shows, music arrived via playlist-add or surprise drop — frictionless and weightless. A show re-attached ceremony to a debut.
  2. It made curation a star vehicle again. Algorithms personalize; DJs persuade. The celebrity show let an artist's taste itself become content.
  3. It was another Toronto export. Just as the city's sound conquered streaming, its artists defined streaming radio's grammar — further proof that the 6's real product was never just songs, but the whole architecture around them.

Frequently asked questions

What is OVO Sound Radio?

Drake's show on Apple Music's Beats 1, launched in 2015 — a platform for premieres, OVO signings, and guest curation that debuted “Hotline Bling” to the world.

What is Memento Mori?

The Weeknd's Apple Music show, launched 2018 — a dark-curated mix series featuring deep cuts and unreleased tracks tied to his album eras.

Why did celebrity radio shows matter in the streaming era?

They restored the “event” to music premieres and made an artist's personal curation into content — a human counterweight to algorithmic playlists.
