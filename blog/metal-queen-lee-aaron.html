“Metal Queen”: Lee Aaron’s Coronation, and the Door She Kicked Open

In 1984, a 21-year-old from Belleville, Ontario — born Karen Lynn Greening — released a song that named its own mythology. “Metal Queen” was Lee Aaron's coronation anthem: a fist-pumping declaration over twin guitars that anointed its singer as the reigning monarch of Canadian metal, and forced a male-dominated scene to make room.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

The song and the image

“Metal Queen” arrived on the album of the same name, and it was savvy in ways the genre rarely managed: the title track served as persona and brand in one — Aaron in leathers and studs on the cover, wielding a voice that could genuinely sing the genre's demands rather than merely survive them. The production is classic mid-80s metal — sharp riffs, chant-ready chorus, guitar-hero solos — and the video, in steady MuchMusic rotation, made her unavoidable. Critics argued about the image; nobody argued about the pipes. Aaron had range, grit, and stage command, and “Metal Queen” put all of it on the national stage at once.

The career beyond the crown

Aaron's real achievement is what came after refusing to stay crowned in one genre. Bodyrock (1989) delivered the bigger commercial moment — the title track and “Whatcha Do to My Body” crossing to mainstream Canadian rock radio — while later albums moved confidently into blues and jazz, where her voice found new gears entirely. It's one of the more graceful second acts in Canadian rock: the “Metal Queen” who outgrew the title and kept singing circles around every box it tried to hold her in.

The legacy

Every Canadian woman who has headbanged her way to a stage — from the 80s metal underground to today's hard-rock revival — walks a corridor Lee Aaron cleared. She proved a woman could front Canadian metal not as a novelty but as a contender, and she did it at the genre's loudest, most macho moment. “Metal Queen” still rips on any playlist that takes it seriously, and Aaron, still performing and recording, remains the title's rightful holder.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Lee Aaron?

A Canadian rock singer born Karen Lynn Greening in Belleville, Ontario — dubbed the “Metal Queen” after her 1984 signature song and album.

What was Lee Aaron’s biggest hit?

In commercial terms, “Bodyrock” (1989) and “Whatcha Do to My Body”; in cultural terms, “Metal Queen” remains the defining record.

What is Lee Aaron doing now?

She continues to perform and record, having moved from metal into rock, blues, and jazz across a four-decade career.
