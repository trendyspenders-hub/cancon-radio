Michael Bublé: How Burnaby's Hockey Kid Brought Crooning Back

Every Canadian over thirty knows the outline: the Burnaby kid who wanted to be a hockey player, discovered the standards through his grandfather's records, and sang his way to becoming the biggest-selling crooner of the modern era. But the Bublé story is stranger and harder than the outline suggests — busking, self-financed albums, a prime minister's wedding, and a career built one ballroom at a time.

BY CANCON RADIO4 MIN READA TIMELINE

The timeline

1975–1995Burnaby and the grandpa years

Michael Steven Bublé is born in Burnaby, BC, on September 9, 1975, to an Italian-Canadian fishing family. Hockey is the dream — the Canucks, specifically. But his grandfather, a plumber named Demetrio, fills the house with the records of Sinatra, Bennett, and Ella, and young Michael absorbs the Songbook as a birthright. He sings at family events, wins local talent shows, and spends his teens playing cruise ships and hotel lounges, learning stagecraft the brutal way: four sets a night, six nights a week.

1996–2002The independent grind

Nobody signs a crooner in the grunge era. So Bublé finances his own albums — First Dance (1996), Babalu (2001), Dream (2002) — and builds a regional following in British Columbia, singing standards and show tunes to rooms that sometimes weren't listening. He acts (a memorable stint on The X-Files), he waits tables, he keeps singing. The hustle is the story: a decade of dues paid in full.

2001The wedding that changed everything

Bublé performs at the wedding of former prime minister Brian Mulroney's daughter — singing "Mack the Knife" — and Mulroney's aide passes his independent CDs to producer David Foster. Foster, the hitmaker behind Celine and Whitney, hears what the rooms have been hearing: a singer with genuine swing feel and a fan's devotion to the material. He signs Bublé to his 143 label.

2003The debut

Michael Bublé (2003) arrives with Foster's gleaming production and instantly separates Bublé from the lounge circuit: "How Can You Mend a Broken Heart," "Kissing a Fool," "Sway," "Moondance" — the record goes multi-platinum worldwide and makes him, at 28, the new face of an old art form.

2005–2009"Home" and superstardom

It's Time (2005) delivers the signature song Bublé co-wrote — "Home," a ballad of longing that became a wedding and radio standard — plus a gorgeous "Feeling Good" and "Save the Last Dance for Me." The album sells millions. Call Me Irresponsible (2007) adds "Everything" and "Lost." Then Crazy Love (2009) seals the empire: "Haven't Met You Yet" is a global top-five, and the album tops charts in a dozen countries.

2011–2016The Christmas king

Christmas (2011) becomes one of the best-selling holiday albums ever recorded — a perennial that returns to charts every December like weather — and Bublé settles into an almost Sinatra-like seasonal ubiquity. To Be Loved (2013) and Nobody but Me (2016) keep the machine humming.

2016–2018The hardest chapter

Bublé's world stops when his eldest son, Noah, is diagnosed with cancer at age three. He cancels everything; the family moves through treatment together; Noah recovers. Bublé has spoken about the ordeal with characteristic openness, and his return to performing carried a new gravity — the entertainer who now knows what the songs are actually about.

2018–presentThe elder crooner

Love (2018) and Higher (2022) — the latter earning the Grammy for Best Traditional Pop Vocal Album — confirm the second act: a singer in his late 40s with the catalog, the Vegas residencies, and the multi-generational audience that makes him arguably the most durable pop entertainer Canada has produced. More than 75 million records sold; arena tours; a Christmas special that's practically a national institution.

Why Bublé worked when everyone said crooning was dead

  1. He sings like a fan, not a museum. The Songbook is alive in his hands — swing, humor, and sweat, not reverence.
  2. He writes his own standards. "Home," "Everything," "Haven't Met You Yet" — originals that sit naturally beside the classics.
  3. He never stopped working rooms. A decade of lounges before fame made him bulletproof on stage; the arena show is just a bigger room.

The Canadian angle, in full

Strip away the global machinery and Bublé's story is a British Columbia one: Burnaby rec centres, Vancouver Island fishing boats, the Canucks dream, and a grandfather's record player. He's remained pointedly Canadian through it all — the Christmas specials feature Canadian guests, the causes he supports are largely Canadian children's charities, and his stage patter still drops hockey references that only half the arena understands. For a guy who sings like a mid-century American, his citizenship is loud, proud, and distinctly ours.

Frequently asked questions

What is Michael Bublé's biggest hit?

"Haven't Met You Yet" (2009) is his biggest original; "Feeling Good" and "Home" are his signature recordings; his Christmas album (2011) is among the best-selling holiday albums ever.

Who discovered Michael Bublé?

Producer David Foster signed him in 2002 after receiving Bublé's self-financed CDs via former prime minister Brian Mulroney's office, following Bublé's performance at a Mulroney family wedding.

Where is Michael Bublé from?

Burnaby, British Columbia. He grew up in an Italian-Canadian family and dreamed of playing hockey for the Vancouver Canucks before music took over.
