Backxwash: Horror-Rap's Polaris-Winning Vision

The greatest Canadian hip-hop album of the 2020s so far arrived from the last place the industry looked: Backxwash — the Zambian-born, Montreal-based, trans horrorcore artist whose God Has Nothing to Do With This Leave Me Out of It (2020) won the Polaris Music Prize and redefined what the country's rap could be: occult, autobiographical, sonically terrifying, and — against every expectation the genre imposes? — keep it light: occult, autobiographical, sonically terrifying, and absolutely undeniable.

BY CANCON RADIO2 MIN READAN ORAL HISTORY

Chapter one: the Montreal underground

Ashanti Mutinta arrived in Montreal from Zambia as a teenager and built Backxwash in the city's underground (see our Montreal features) — the DIY wing where identities and sounds get forged without permission. The early records established the elements: horror-movie production (occult samples, doom-laden beats), confessional writing (faith, family, queerness, trauma — the autobiography rendered as nightmare), and a flow that could switch from whisper to? — keep it light: confessional writing, and a technical command that made the darkness craft, not costume. The underground took notice first: this was something new, arriving fully formed.

Chapter two: the Polaris coronation (2020)

God Has Nothing to Do With This — the 2020 record — was the coronation: an album-length descent? — keep it light: an album-length séance of faith and fury, universally acclaimed and crowned with the Polaris Music Prize (see our Polaris feature) — the award's statement that Canadian music's future sounded like this: trans, Black, immigrant, occult, Montreal, and hip-hop at its most artistically ambitious. The win resonated far beyond the genre (see our trans-and-nonbinary feature): it announced that the margins of Canadian music — where Backxwash built — were the new center of its artistic ambition.

Chapter three: the vision continues

The years since have deepened rather than repeated: subsequent records extending the horrorcore universe (the production ever more cinematic, the writing ever more precise), the international underground's reverence growing (the collaborations and the cult crossing borders), and the role — claimed and exercised — of visionary: an artist whose example expanded the field's sense of the possible. Backxwash makes rap that sounds like no one else on earth, from a city the world keeps underestimating, carrying identities the industry kept marginal — and the Polaris sits on the shelf as the receipt. Horror was never the genre. It was the door.

Why she matters

She matters because every canon needs its expander — the artist who walks in from the underground and changes the building's dimensions — and Backxwash is Canadian hip-hop's: the proof that the country's rap future is being written in Montreal, in trans experience, in darkness that illuminates. God has nothing to do with it. She did it herself.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Backxwash?

Ashanti Mutinta — a Zambian-born, Montreal-based trans hip-hop artist whose horrorcore has won international acclaim, including the 2020 Polaris Music Prize.

What is Backxwash’s Polaris-winning album?

God Has Nothing to Do With This Leave Me Out of It (2020).

What is Backxwash’s sound?

Horrorcore: occult, cinematic production; confessional, autobiographical writing; technical rap command — darkness as craft.
